## The basics

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is Lumovi free?">
    Yes. Lumovi is free and open source under the [Apache 2.0 license](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/blob/main/LICENSE), on your desktop and in your cluster. There are no accounts and no telemetry. It's made in spare time; if it saves you time, you can [sponsor its development](https://github.com/sponsors/kotapeter).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Should I use the desktop app or run it in my cluster?">
    It's the same app either way, so pick what fits:

    | | Desktop app | In your cluster |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Who uses it | You | Your whole team, in a browser |
    | Clusters | Every cluster in your kubeconfig | The one it runs in, or a [fleet](/server/fleet) of many |
    | Credentials | Your kubeconfig | Each person signs in |
    | Installing | An app per person | One Helm chart, nothing for users |
    | Port forwarding | Yes | No |
    | Terminals with kubectl pointed at the cluster | Yes | No |
    | [AI assistants](/assistants/overview) | Yes, with a token on your computer | Yes, [signed in through Lumovi](/assistants/server) |
    | Charts from your computer | Yes | No |

    Plenty of teams use both. See [Install the desktop app](/get-started/desktop) and [Run it in your cluster](/get-started/in-your-cluster).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need kubectl?">
    No. Lumovi talks to the API server itself. It still shows the equivalent `kubectl` command for every change, so you can learn it, copy it or script it. [Helm](https://helm.sh) is needed only to change Helm releases; looking at them needs nothing.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which Kubernetes versions does it support?">
    Kubernetes 1.25 or later. From 1.27, Lumovi also explains fields from the cluster's own OpenAPI schema. When it runs in your cluster, signing in with a token needs 1.28 or later.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it work with EKS, GKE, AKS and other managed clusters?">
    Yes. Lumovi reads your kubeconfig the way `kubectl` does, with client certificates, tokens and exec credential plugins like `gke-gcloud-auth-plugin`, `aws eks get-token` and `kubelogin`. If `kubectl` can reach a cluster with your kubeconfig, Lumovi can too. See [Connecting clusters](/clusters/connect).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How is it different from other Kubernetes tools?">
    Lumovi is built around one question: is this cluster healthy, and if not, where should I look first? So it shows problems before everything else, sets usage against capacity, and keeps the details one click away. It's neither a terminal nor a wall of tables.

    When you change something, it's careful: it asks the cluster what you're allowed to do, shows the `kubectl` command, checks edits with a dry run, and offers undo. And it's the same app on your desktop and in your cluster, so a team can share it without installing anything.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Your data and your clusters

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does Lumovi change my kubeconfig?">
    No. It reads your kubeconfig and never writes to it. Managing kubeconfig files, like adding contexts or signing in to cloud providers, is deliberately left to the tools made for it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it collect any data?">
    No. There's no telemetry and no account. Its [audit log](/audit/overview) stays on your computer. The desktop app talks to your clusters, and to a few other places only when you use what needs them, like update checks and chart search. See [Privacy and security](/reference/privacy-and-security) for the full list.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I use it without an internet connection?">
    Lumovi needs to reach your clusters, and nothing else. Without the internet, everything works except looking for new versions, searching Artifact Hub, and fetching charts from public repositories.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it change things by accident?">
    It's built not to. Changes are always explicit: an action, a dialog that names the cluster and shows the command, and a confirmation. Destructive dialogs start on **Cancel**, and risky deletes, or anything in a cluster named like production, ask you to type the name first. For a cluster you only want to look at, turn on [read-only mode](/changes/read-only). See [Changing things safely](/changes/safely).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Lumovi keep a record of what's done?">
    Yes: an [audit log](/audit/overview) of what was done through Lumovi, by whom, from where, and how it went, with the `kubectl` command for each change and how each AI assistant's change was approved. Never Secrets' values or tokens. The desktop app keeps yours on your computer, for 90 days. Lumovi in your cluster keeps one for the team: people see their own events, its auditors see everyone's, and it can send them to your log collector or SIEM. Its events are chained, and Lumovi can [check](/audit/verify) them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can my team let people do less through Lumovi than their RBAC allows?">
    Yes, when it runs in your cluster. Its admins say what everyone gets, give teams more where they work, and hold anyone back where it matters, like no shells or Secret values in production. Lumovi's server holds to it, and never lets anyone do more than RBAC. Each person sees theirs in [Your access](/clusters/your-access). See [Access for your team](/server/access).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it work behind a company proxy?">
    Yes. Lumovi goes through the proxy in `HTTPS_PROXY`, `HTTP_PROXY` and `NO_PROXY`, as `kubectl` and `helm` do, and trusts the certificate authorities your computer trusts, like the one a proxy that inspects HTTPS signs with. The desktop app takes the proxy from your shell. In your cluster, the chart's `proxy` and `extraCA` set them. See [Proxies and certificates](/desktop/network), or for a server, [Proxies and certificate authorities](/server/network).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can IT set what Lumovi allows on our computers?">
    Yes, with a [policy](/desktop/policy) deployed by your MDM, Group Policy, Intune or configuration management: clusters read-only, AI assistants off or limited, updates left to IT, and the proxy and certificate authorities to use. What it sets is locked, and the app says "Set by your organization". It sets what Lumovi does: what people may do in a cluster is still its RBAC's to say.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What permissions do I need?">
    Only what you want to do. Lumovi acts with your own credentials and RBAC, so a read-only `view` role is enough to look around. It asks the cluster what you may do before offering an action, and says why when you can't. See [Permissions](/clusters/permissions).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Using it

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I work with several clusters?">
    Yes. The desktop app lists every context in your kubeconfig on its start screen, with whether each one answers. Switch between them from the cluster menu at the top of the sidebar, or with <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>. Each cluster remembers its own namespace, read-only setting and usage history source.

    Lumovi [in your cluster](/server/overview) shows the one cluster it runs in, unless you set it up as a [fleet](/server/fleet).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can my team see many clusters in a browser?">
    Yes, with a [fleet](/server/fleet): one Lumovi for many clusters, private ones too. People sign in once, with single sign-on or through a proxy, and its home page sums up every cluster, the ones that need attention first. In each cluster, they see and change what their own RBAC there allows: Lumovi impersonates them, or passes their own token on.

    A fleet runs in a cluster with the Helm chart, or without Kubernetes, with Docker or on a platform like [Sevalla](/server/fleet/sevalla). For clusters it can't reach, an [agent](/server/fleet/agents) in each one dials out to it. See [Clusters in a fleet](/clusters/fleet) for using one.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it handle big clusters?">
    Yes. Lists load in chunks of 500 and are paginated and virtualized, so thousands of pods stay smooth. They stop at 5,000 objects by default, with a note saying so; a label selector narrows a list on the server. Busy lists refresh less often.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it support my custom resources?">
    Yes, every kind the cluster serves, found through API discovery. They get the columns `kubectl get` prints and a status read from their conditions. Views for popular projects (cert-manager, Argo CD, Flux, Karpenter and more) add better columns, links and actions, and each project's add-on gives it an entry in the sidebar, with everything it runs in one list. You can write your own views and add-ons too. See [Custom resources](/custom-resources/overview).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Lumovi work with AI assistants?">
    Yes, in the desktop app and on your team's Lumovi. Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code and other assistants that speak MCP can read your clusters through Lumovi, and find what's wrong. They ask before they change anything: each change is checked by the cluster, then waits in Lumovi with its diff and its `kubectl` command, for you to approve or reject. Lumovi has no AI of its own and needs no API key: it's a tool your assistant uses, with your credentials, and you say [what it may do](/assistants/permissions), and where: what it sees, whether its changes ask you, and whether it reads Secrets' values, env values and logs. In the desktop app, it's off until you turn it on. On a server, assistants sign in through Lumovi in your browser, and act as you. See [AI assistants](/assistants/overview), and [On a server](/assistants/server).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where does usage history come from?">
    From a Prometheus or VictoriaMetrics in the cluster, which Lumovi finds by itself and reaches through the API server with your own credentials. Live usage comes from metrics-server. See [Usage history](/metrics/usage-history).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is there a light theme?">
    Yes. Lumovi follows your system, or you can pick light or dark from the theme button at the bottom of the sidebar, or with <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The project

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Who makes Lumovi?">
    Lumovi was started by [Peter Kota](https://github.com/kotapeter), first as a tool for work: an app to open a cluster and know in a second whether it's healthy. It's developed in the open with its contributors on [GitHub](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I used KubeStacks. What changes?">
    Lumovi is the same app, with a new name. Lumovi 1.0.0 has everything KubeStacks had. A few things don't carry over:

    - **Installed copies don't update into Lumovi.** [Download Lumovi](https://lumovi.dev/#download) and install it. It's a separate app, so KubeStacks stays until you remove it.
    - **Settings start over.** Your theme, read-only clusters, usage history sources and the rest stay in KubeStacks' own folder, which Lumovi doesn't read. Your kubeconfig is read as before.
    - **Your own views** go in `~/.lumovi/views` instead of `~/.kubestacks/views`, and start with `apiVersion: lumovi.dev/v1alpha1` instead of `kubestacks.dev/v1alpha1`. Lumovi doesn't use a file with the old `apiVersion`, and lists it under **API resources**.
    - **Environment variables** start with `LUMOVI_` instead of `KUBESTACKS_`.
    - **In your cluster**, the image is `ghcr.io/lumovi/lumovi` and the chart `oci://ghcr.io/lumovi/charts/lumovi`, and the server's settings are `LUMOVI_*` variables. See [Install Lumovi in your cluster](/server/install).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How can I help?">
    - **Report what's wrong, or what's missing**, in an [issue](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/issues).
    - **Add support for a tool you run.** An add-on and views for it are YAML, checked against the tool's CRDs, with no code to write. It's an easy and welcome first contribution. See [Built-in views](/custom-resources/built-in-views) for what Lumovi has, and [Build from source](/reference/build-from-source#contributing) for how to add one.
    - **Contribute.** Pull requests are welcome; for anything bigger than a small fix, open an issue first. See [Build from source](/reference/build-from-source).
    - **Sponsor** its development on [GitHub Sponsors](https://github.com/sponsors/kotapeter). The desktop app has a link in **Help → Sponsor Lumovi…** too.
    - **Star it** on [GitHub](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi), so others find it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where do I report a security problem?">
    Privately, please, by email to [security@lumovi.dev](mailto:security@lumovi.dev), or through a [GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/security/advisories/new). See [Reporting a vulnerability](/reference/privacy-and-security#reporting-a-vulnerability).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I check a release is genuine?">
    Each installer, the image and the chart is built by Lumovi's release workflow on GitHub, which attests it, and lists what it's made of in a bill of materials. The image and the chart are signed with cosign too. Check an installer with `gh attestation verify`: see [Verify your download](/get-started/desktop#verify-your-download). For the image and the chart, see [Check a release](/server/security#check-a-release).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
