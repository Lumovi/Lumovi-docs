A plugin gathers the kinds of one tool, like Flux, cert-manager or Karpenter, under one entry in the sidebar. Its page lists everything of those kinds together, the way [Workloads](/explore/workloads) does: one list, what's failing first, and a tab for each kind.

Lumovi ships plugins for 32 tools, from Argo CD to Velero, and shows the ones your cluster runs. However many CRDs a cluster has, the tools on it are a click away. See [Included plugins](/custom-resources/included-plugins) for every plugin and what it covers.

## In the sidebar

A plugin shows up once the cluster serves one of its tool's kinds. Lumovi looks at what the cluster serves again every minute, so a tool you just installed appears on its own, and one you removed goes away.

- **Under Plugins.** Most plugins sit in a section of their own, by name, between **Pinned** and **Custom resources**.
- **With Kubernetes' own kinds.** Plugins that extend what Kubernetes does sit at the end of that section: **Cilium**, **Gateway API**, **Linkerd** and **Traefik** under **Network**, **Sealed Secrets** under **Configuration**, and **Longhorn**, **Rook Ceph** and **Volume Snapshots** under **Storage**.

A plugin's entry stays highlighted on its page and on its kinds' lists. Its kinds are left out of the kinds you opened lately, under **Custom resources**, since they're a click away already. To keep one of them at hand anyway, [pin it](/custom-resources/overview#a-short-sidebar-however-many-crds).

The command palette (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>) finds plugins too, marked **Plugin**: by their name, or by the name of any of their kinds, so typing `certificates` offers **cert-manager**. (⌘ is Ctrl on Windows and Linux.)

## The plugin's page

### Tabs

**All** comes first, then a tab for each of the tool's kinds that the cluster serves, in the plugin's order, with how many objects it has. A kind's tab is its usual list, with its view's columns and actions, and the plugin's tabs stay above it. When there are more tabs than fit, they scroll sideways.

Counts and lists follow the [namespace](/clusters/namespaces) you picked. Kinds without namespaces are always the whole cluster's, and when all of a tool's kinds are, like Karpenter's, the namespace picker says **Cluster-wide**.

### Everything in one list

**All** lists every object of the tool's kinds:

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | The object's name, with its namespace when you're looking at all of them |
| **Type** | Its kind, with the kind's icon |
| **Status** | Its [health](/explore/health), from its kind's [view](/custom-resources/overview#views) or the usual conventions |
| **Message** | What the status says beyond its label, like why it failed. Shown when any object has one. |
| **Age** | How long ago it was created |

The list sorts by status, so what's failing comes first: <span className="lumovi-status critical">Failing</span>, then <span className="lumovi-status warning">Warning</span>, <span className="lumovi-status progressing">In progress</span>, <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Healthy</span> and <span className="lumovi-status neutral">Inactive</span>. Click any column to sort by it instead.

- **Health.** The chips above the list (**Failing**, **Warning**, **In progress**, **Healthy**, **Inactive**) show only objects in that state, with how many there are. Pick several to combine them.
- **Filter.** Press <kbd>/</kbd> and type. **Filter Flux** (named after the tool) matches names, namespaces, kinds, labels written as `key=value`, and what the status says.
- **Label selector.** Type a selector like `app=web` and press <kbd>↵</kbd>. The cluster filters every kind's list with it.

Click a row to open its details, with what its kind's view adds: details, links, tabs for related objects, and actions. To act on several objects at once, pick rows with <kbd>X</kbd> or Shift-click, and choose from the bar that appears. See [Several at once](/changes/bulk).

### When something's missing

- **Nothing yet.** With no objects, the page says **Nothing from Flux in shop** (or **in this cluster**), and which kinds would show up here.
- **A kind it couldn't list.** When you can't list one of the kinds, a line above the list says **Couldn't list**, which kind, and why. The other kinds still show.
- **A long list.** Lists that are too long to load whole are cut short, and the page says some objects may be missing. Choose a namespace, or use **Filter by label**.
- **A tool the cluster doesn't have.** A plugin's page opened from a link, in a cluster without the tool, says **This cluster doesn't have it**: none of its kinds are served there.

## Karpenter

[Karpenter](https://karpenter.sh)'s plugin opens on an overview of its own instead of a list: its node pools against their limits, the nodes they launched and the ones launching, the mix of instance types, capacity types and zones, what's being disrupted, and the pods waiting for a node. Its first tab is **Overview**, and its kinds' tabs (NodePools, NodeClaims, and the node classes) follow.

The overview counts only the nodes Karpenter launched: the ones labeled `karpenter.sh/nodepool`.

### The four numbers

| Tile | Shows | Below it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Node pools ready** | Ready node pools, of all of them | **All ready**, or how many aren't |
| **Nodes ready** | Ready nodes, of all the nodes Karpenter launched | How many are on spot, like *2 of 3 on spot*, or how many aren't ready |
| **Launching** | Node claims launched but not yet registered as nodes | How many pods are waiting for a node, or **No pods waiting** |
| **Being replaced** | Node claims terminating, being replaced or drifted | **Drifted or terminating**, or **Nothing to replace** |

Each tile opens the list behind it. **Node pools ready** and **Nodes ready** open filtered to what isn't ready when something isn't, and **Nodes ready** opens Nodes with the label selector `karpenter.sh/nodepool`. **Launching** opens the node claims, filtered to the ones in progress while some are launching.

### Node pools

Each node pool, by name, with its status, how many nodes it has, and how much of its CPU and memory limits its nodes take, like *24 of 64 cores*. A node pool without a limit says *no limit*, with a dashed track.

Under its name, a node pool says what it launches: its capacity types and instance families, like *Spot, on-demand · m6i, c6i*. These come from its requirements on `karpenter.sh/capacity-type` (on-demand when it has none), and on `karpenter.k8s.aws/instance-family`, `karpenter.k8s.aws/instance-category`, `karpenter.azure.com/sku-family` or `node.kubernetes.io/instance-type`, the first it has. A node pool that isn't ready shows why instead.

### Nodes

Karpenter's nodes, grouped by node pool, each with its instance type, capacity type and zone. With [metrics-server](/metrics/live-usage), each has meters for its CPU and memory use; without it, its status and how many cores it has. Node claims still on their way are listed with their node pool, as **Launching**, with how long ago they started.

### Node mix

How many nodes there are of each **instance type**, **capacity type** and **zone**, the most common first.

### Disruption

The node claims Karpenter is replacing, or could do without, with how long they've been that way:

| Status | Means |
| --- | --- |
| <span className="lumovi-status progressing">Terminating</span> | It's being deleted |
| <span className="lumovi-status progressing">Being replaced</span> | Karpenter tainted its node `karpenter.sh/disrupted`, to replace it |
| <span className="lumovi-status warning">Drifted</span> | It no longer matches its node pool or node class; Karpenter's message says what changed |
| <span className="lumovi-status neutral">Consolidatable</span> | Its pods fit elsewhere, or on a cheaper node |

When there are none, it says *Nothing is being disrupted.*

### Waiting for a node

The pods the scheduler couldn't place anywhere, which Karpenter launches nodes for, with the scheduler's message. When there are none: *No pods are waiting for a node.*

**Nodes** (for each node pool), **Disruption** and **Waiting for a node** show six rows, with **Show all** for the rest. Click anything to open it.

The overview reads node pools, node claims and nodes, and pods in every namespace, so it needs `list` on all four. A card whose list you can't read says why, and the others still show.

## Every plugin on one page

**All plugins**, last under **Plugins** in the sidebar, lists every plugin Lumovi has: the ones it comes with, and the files in your folder. It opens by saying what a plugin is:

> A plugin teaches Lumovi a tool’s kinds: their columns, status, details and actions. Plugins are data. They can’t run code, and they change objects only with the patches and the objects they spell out, each shown here. A plugin has no access of its own: what it reads and changes, it does as you, and the cluster refuses what you may not do. Lumovi’s come inside Lumovi; yours are your own files.

Each plugin is a row:

- **Plugin**: its name, its version and the kinds it shows (three, then how many more), with **Yours** on one from your folder.
- **On** the cluster: **On this cluster: in the sidebar** where the cluster serves one of its kinds and it has a place there, **On this cluster** for one with views only, or **Not on this cluster**. One of yours with the name of one of Lumovi's says **In use in place of Lumovi’s Flux**, and Lumovi's own row says **Not in use: your flux.yaml is used in its place, all of it.**
- **From**: **Lumovi**, which came with this Lumovi, or **Yours**, with the file's path.

**All**, **On this cluster**, **Yours** and **Not in use** narrow the list, and **Search plugins** finds one by its name or its file's. At the foot is where yours go, like *35 of 35 shown. Yours go in ~/.lumovi/plugins. One with the name of one of Lumovi’s is used in its place, all of it.*

### What a plugin asks for

Open a row to see what the plugin asks for, read from its file in Lumovi's words. Nothing there is the plugin's own account of itself.

- **May change and create**: only the kinds it shows, by name, and the API groups they're all in; what its actions create, each one of its own kinds; and what no plugin can: what's the cluster's to set on an object (its owners, finalizers, name or namespace), and Kubernetes' own kinds. A plugin without actions says **Nothing. It has no actions.**
- **May read**: its own kinds' objects, whose fields its columns, status and details show; the kinds of Kubernetes' it [shows](/reference/plugin-format#kubernetes’-own-kinds) and can't change; the kinds its related lists list beside an object, picked by their labels or fields; and the kinds it links to by name. It ends: "It reads no logs and no Secret’s value, and sends nothing anywhere but to this cluster."
- **Says what manages workloads**: where a view says [what it manages](/reference/plugin-format#what-it-manages), every object of those kinds in the cluster you may list (or, where you can't, in the namespaces you're looking at), to say which of them manages each workload, by labels and annotations the workload carries under the tool's own groups.
- **Its actions, and what each sends**: each action with the kinds it's on, whether it **asks first** or **runs at once**, and its patch as the file writes it, or the object it creates under **Creates**. What's filled in later is shown as what it is: `(now)`, `(what you choose)`, `(from the object)`, and `(removed)` for a field a patch takes away. An action's undo is shown under it as **Undo sends:**, and one that patches an object's status says so.

### When a file isn't in use

A file with a problem is a row too, by its file's name: **Not in use. 2 problems to fix in the file.**, with each problem under it, saying where in the file it is. Where the file has the name of one of Lumovi's plugins, the row adds **Lumovi’s Flux is shown instead**. **API resources** says only that there are problems, **A plugin couldn’t be used.** or how many, with **See Plugins** to come here. Every message is in the [plugin format](/reference/plugin-format#problems).

## Your own plugins

A plugin is one short YAML file, a document of kind `Plugin`, in `~/.lumovi/plugins`. This one gives two kinds of your own a place in the sidebar:

```yaml platform.yaml
apiVersion: lumovi.dev/v1beta1
kind: Plugin
metadata:
  name: platform
spec:
  version: 1.0.0
  needs:
    lumovi: 1.22.0
  groups: [platform.example.com]
  label: Platform
  icon: database
  kinds:
    - { group: platform.example.com, kind: Database }
    - { group: platform.example.com, kind: DatabaseBackup }
```

Press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>R</kbd>, and **Platform** appears under **Plugins**, with a tab for each kind.

- **`version`**, **`needs`** and **`groups`** say what it is: its own version, the oldest Lumovi it works with, and the API groups its kinds are in.
- **`kinds`** are the tabs, in order. `kind: '*'` with a `group` is every kind of that group, each with a tab.
- **`label`** is its name in the sidebar, and needed with `kinds`.
- **`icon`** is one of the [icons](/reference/plugin-format#icons).
- **`category`** puts it at the end of one of Kubernetes' sections instead: `cluster`, `network`, `config` or `storage`.
- **`groups`** hold every kind it names. A plugin is for a tool's kinds: Kubernetes' own can't be under its place, and a plugin can only [show](/reference/plugin-format#kubernetes’-own-kinds) the ones Lumovi has no page for.

A place in the sidebar only groups kinds. How each kind looks, its columns, status and actions, comes from a view, which the plugin holds under `views:`. See [Write a plugin](/custom-resources/write-a-plugin).

### Replacing one of Lumovi's

A kind is one plugin's, so a plugin of yours can't show a kind one of Lumovi's has, unless it takes that plugin's place. Give yours the same `metadata.name` as Lumovi's, like `flux` or `cert-manager` ([Included plugins](/custom-resources/included-plugins) gives each one's name). Yours is then used in its place, all of it: Lumovi's place in the sidebar and every one of its views go.

```yaml flux.yaml
apiVersion: lumovi.dev/v1beta1
kind: Plugin
metadata:
  name: flux
spec:
  version: 1.0.0
  needs:
    lumovi: 1.22.0
  groups: [kustomize.toolkit.fluxcd.io, helm.toolkit.fluxcd.io]
  label: GitOps
  icon: git-branch
  kinds:
    - { group: kustomize.toolkit.fluxcd.io, kind: Kustomization }
    - { group: helm.toolkit.fluxcd.io, kind: HelmRelease }
```

This one has no views, so its two kinds get the API server's columns, and Flux's other kinds are no longer in the sidebar. To change a little, start from all of it: copy the file from [Lumovi's plugins](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/tree/main/plugins) and edit the copy. **API resources** names your file under **Shown by**, and **All plugins** says on its row that it's in use in place of Lumovi's.

Under any other name, a plugin of yours that shows one of those kinds isn't used, and the problem says what to do: "certificates.yaml: Certificate.cert-manager.io is cert-manager’s kind. A plugin of yours named cert-manager takes its place, all of it".

### When something's wrong with it

A plugin is used whole or not at all. If anything in its file is wrong, none of it is used, and [All plugins](#when-a-file-isn’t-in-use) lists everything wrong with it under the file's own row. When that leaves one of Lumovi's in use in place of yours, the row says so: "Lumovi’s Flux is shown instead". Every field, and every message, is in the [plugin format](/reference/plugin-format).

  **In your cluster:** plugins everyone sees go in the chart's `plugins` value. People can't add their own from a browser. See [Plugins for everyone](/server/plugins).
