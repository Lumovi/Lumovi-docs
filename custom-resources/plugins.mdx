A plugin gathers the kinds of one tool, like Flux, cert-manager or Karpenter, under one entry in the sidebar. Its page lists everything of those kinds together, the way [Workloads](/explore/workloads) does: one list, what's failing first, and a tab for each kind.

Lumovi ships plugins for 32 tools, from Argo CD to Velero, and shows the ones your cluster runs. However many CRDs a cluster has, the tools on it are a click away. See [Included plugins](/custom-resources/included-plugins) for every plugin and what it covers.

## In the sidebar

A plugin shows up once the cluster serves one of its tool's kinds. Lumovi looks at what the cluster serves again every minute, so a tool you just installed appears on its own, and one you removed goes away.

- **Under Plugins.** Most plugins sit in a section of their own, by name, between **Pinned** and **Custom resources**.
- **With Kubernetes' own kinds.** Plugins that extend what Kubernetes does sit at the end of that section: **Cilium**, **Gateway API**, **Linkerd** and **Traefik** under **Network**, **Sealed Secrets** under **Configuration**, and **Longhorn**, **Rook Ceph** and **Volume Snapshots** under **Storage**.

A plugin's entry stays highlighted on its page and on its kinds' lists. Its kinds are left out of the kinds you opened lately, under **Custom resources**, since they're a click away already. To keep one of them at hand anyway, [pin it](/custom-resources/overview#a-short-sidebar-however-many-crds).

The command palette (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>) finds plugins too, marked **Plugin**: by their name, or by the name of any of their kinds, so typing `certificates` offers **cert-manager**. (⌘ is Ctrl on Windows and Linux.)

## The plugin's page

### Tabs

**All** comes first, then a tab for each of the tool's kinds that the cluster serves, in the plugin's order, with how many objects it has. A kind's tab is its usual list, with its view's columns and actions, and the plugin's tabs stay above it. When there are more tabs than fit, they scroll sideways.

Counts and lists follow the [namespace](/clusters/namespaces) you picked. Kinds without namespaces are always the whole cluster's, and when all of a tool's kinds are, like Karpenter's, the namespace picker says **Cluster-wide**.

### Everything in one list

**All** lists every object of the tool's kinds:

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | The object's name, with its namespace when you're looking at all of them |
| **Type** | Its kind, with the kind's icon |
| **Status** | Its [health](/explore/health), from its kind's [view](/custom-resources/overview#views) or the usual conventions |
| **Message** | What the status says beyond its label, like why it failed. Shown when any object has one. |
| **Age** | How long ago it was created |

The list sorts by status, so what's failing comes first: <span className="lumovi-status critical">Failing</span>, then <span className="lumovi-status warning">Warning</span>, <span className="lumovi-status progressing">In progress</span>, <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Healthy</span> and <span className="lumovi-status neutral">Inactive</span>. Click any column to sort by it instead.

- **Health.** The chips above the list (**Failing**, **Warning**, **In progress**, **Healthy**, **Inactive**) show only objects in that state, with how many there are. Pick several to combine them.
- **Filter.** Press <kbd>/</kbd> and type. **Filter Flux** (named after the tool) matches names, namespaces, kinds, labels written as `key=value`, and what the status says.
- **Label selector.** Type a selector like `app=web` and press <kbd>↵</kbd>. The cluster filters every kind's list with it.

Click a row to open its details, with what its kind's view adds: details, links, tabs for related objects, and actions. To act on several objects at once, pick rows with <kbd>X</kbd> or Shift-click, and choose from the bar that appears. See [Several at once](/changes/bulk).

### When something's missing

- **Nothing yet.** With no objects, the page says **Nothing from Flux in shop** (or **in this cluster**), and which kinds would show up here.
- **A kind it couldn't list.** When you can't list one of the kinds, a line above the list says **Couldn't list**, which kind, and why. The other kinds still show.
- **A long list.** Lists that are too long to load whole are cut short, and the page says some objects may be missing. Choose a namespace, or use **Filter by label**.
- **A tool the cluster doesn't have.** A plugin's page opened from a link, in a cluster without the tool, says **This cluster doesn't have it**: none of its kinds are served there.

## Pages drawn by a plugin's code

Three of the plugins Lumovi comes with add pages of their own, drawn by their own code: **Karpenter** an [overview](#karpenter), **Argo CD** an overview and a tab on each Application, and **CloudNativePG** an **Overview** and **Backup health**. See [Included plugins](/custom-resources/included-plugins) for what each shows.

That code comes inside Lumovi, from the same [signed release](/custom-resources/included-plugins#where-they-come-from) as the plugins' files, and runs in a sealed box: a place of its own, apart from Lumovi's page, with no network, which can't reach Lumovi's cookies, storage or credentials. It's handed only what its file says it may read, read by Lumovi as you, and it draws with Lumovi's own parts, so it looks like the rest of Lumovi and can't draw anything else. Lumovi stops a page that sends what a plugin can't, or more at once than it may, or stops answering, and in the desktop app one that uses more memory than a plugin may, or more than half of a processor core for half a minute.

A strip at the top of each such page says whose it is: "This page is from the plugin **Argo CD** 1.2.0. Lumovi draws it from its own parts; what it shows and says is the plugin’s.", with **What it may do →**, which opens the plugin's entry under [All plugins](#what-a-plugin-asks-for).

### Changes a page proposes

A plugin's page can't change anything itself. It can propose a change, which Lumovi shows in its own dialog, over its own header where no plugin can draw: what the change does in Lumovi's words, the plugin's options, **What Lumovi will send** as the cluster says it would be (a diff, or the whole object it would create), its `kubectl` command, and whether the cluster accepts it. After every change to the options, Lumovi asks the cluster again, and its button can be pressed a moment after the cluster's answer. Nothing changes until you press it.

What it may propose is in its file, and its entry says it, like *Its pages may propose to change Applications, each change one you confirm in Lumovi.* It's only ever its own kinds, never an object's status, and of its metadata only its labels and annotations, and the name of one it creates. A change comes to at most 16 KiB. A change Lumovi makes for a plugin is in the [audit log](/audit/events#changes-change) as `plugin.change`, without the values you set. Where it can't be made, no dialog opens, and a note says why: in a [read-only](/changes/read-only) cluster, "This cluster is read-only in Lumovi: Argo CD’s change isn’t made here."; where you may not make it, "You may not make Argo CD’s change here."; on a phone, "This isn’t done from a phone. Open Lumovi on a larger screen to change payments."; and where your organization's [policy](/desktop/policy#pluginproposals) says `pluginProposals: off`, "Your organization’s policy doesn’t let plugins propose changes."

### Logs on a plugin's page

A plugin's page can show a pod's logs, where its file says it may (`logs: shown`). Lumovi reads them, as you, and draws them in its own log view, the same as a pod's **Logs** tab: the plugin's code never sees a line. The card says so, like "Shown by Lumovi: Toys’ code never sees them." for a plugin named Toys.

A page shows four logs at once, and a plugin eight, across its pages and tabs. One past that waits, and says why: "Not shown: a plugin’s page shows 4 logs at once. This one starts when another closes." Reading them follows your access to logs, and each read is in the [audit log](/audit/events#access-access) as `logs.read`, its summary ending "shown on the plugin Toys’ page". Where your organization's [policy](/desktop/policy#pluginlogs) says `pluginLogs: off`, the card says "Your organization’s policy doesn’t let plugins show containers’ logs.", and none are read.

None of the plugins Lumovi comes with shows logs yet.

### Links to Lumovi's own places

A plugin's page can link to a pod's **Logs** or **Shell** tab, or to **Port forward** for a pod or a service, where its file says it may, and only for an object it was handed. The link opens Lumovi's own tab or dialog, at the container or port it names. What happens there is what you do: a plugin's page can bring you to a shell, never start one.

A Shell tab opened from a plugin's page says so, like "Opened from Toys’ page" for a plugin named Toys, and waits: "Toys’ page opened this tab. A shell starts only when you press Start: as you, within your access." **Start shell** isn't focused, and <kbd>↵</kbd> doesn't press it. Where the pod has no container by the name the page asked for, the tab says so, "Toys’ page asked for a container named envoy, which this pod doesn’t have.", and opens on its first, as it would anyway. A shell opened this way is in the audit log as any other, its summary ending "from the plugin Toys’ page".

None of the plugins Lumovi comes with links anywhere yet. A plugin's [`links`](/reference/plugin-format#spec) say where it may.

### Turn a plugin's code off

Open a plugin under **All plugins**, and turn off **Run its code**. Under it is what its code does, like *Draws its overview. It reads Nodes and Pods as you, and changes nothing.* Off, its pages and tabs say "You turned off its code. Turn it on in Plugins to see this page." with **Open Plugins**, and nothing of its code is handed to Lumovi's page or run. Its kinds, their lists, and the actions its file spells out stay. It's your own switch: on a server, kept for you alone.

Where Lumovi has an overview of its own for the plugin, as it has for Karpenter, that's shown instead: *Its pages don’t run: you turned off its code. Lumovi shows its own overview of Karpenter instead.*

It can also be off for you:

- **On a server**, where its administrator turned plugins' code off with [`pluginCode`](/server/plugins#plugins’-code): "This server doesn’t run plugins’ code." The switch is off and locked, with **Turned off on this server.**
- **On your organization's computers**, where their [policy](/desktop/policy#plugins) says `no-code`: "Your organization’s policy doesn’t let plugins run code." The switch is off and locked, with **Turned off by your organization’s policy.**

Turning it on or off is in the audit log as `plugin.code-switch`.

## Karpenter

[Karpenter](https://karpenter.sh)'s plugin opens on an overview of its own instead of a list: its node pools against their limits, the nodes they launched and the ones launching, the mix of instance types, capacity types and zones, what's being disrupted, and the pods waiting for a node. Its first tab is **Overview**, and its kinds' tabs (NodePools, NodeClaims, and the node classes) follow.

The overview is drawn by the plugin's [code](#pages-drawn-by-a-plugin’s-code). Wherever that doesn't run, Lumovi shows its own overview of Karpenter in its place, with the same cards.

The overview counts only the nodes Karpenter launched: the ones labeled `karpenter.sh/nodepool`.

### The four numbers

| Tile | Shows | Below it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Node pools ready** | Ready node pools, of all of them | **All ready**, or how many aren't |
| **Nodes ready** | Ready nodes, of all the nodes Karpenter launched | How many are on spot, like *2 of 3 on spot*, or how many aren't ready |
| **Launching** | Node claims launched but not yet registered as nodes | How many pods are waiting for a node, or **No pods waiting** |
| **Being replaced** | Node claims terminating, being replaced or drifted | **Drifted or terminating**, or **Nothing to replace** |

Each tile opens the list behind it. **Node pools ready** and **Nodes ready** open filtered to what isn't ready when something isn't, and **Nodes ready** opens Nodes with the label selector `karpenter.sh/nodepool`. **Launching** opens the node claims, filtered to the ones in progress while some are launching.

### Node pools

Each node pool, by name, with its status, how many nodes it has, and how much of its CPU and memory limits its nodes take, like *24 of 64 cores*. A node pool without a limit says *no limit*, with a dashed track.

Under its name, a node pool says what it launches: its capacity types and instance families, like *Spot, on-demand · m6i, c6i*. These come from its requirements on `karpenter.sh/capacity-type` (on-demand when it has none), and on `karpenter.k8s.aws/instance-family`, `karpenter.k8s.aws/instance-category`, `karpenter.azure.com/sku-family` or `node.kubernetes.io/instance-type`, the first it has. A node pool that isn't ready shows why instead.

### Nodes

Karpenter's nodes, grouped by node pool, each with its instance type, capacity type and zone. With [metrics-server](/metrics/live-usage), each has meters for its CPU and memory use; without it, its status and how many cores it has. Node claims still on their way are listed with their node pool, as **Launching**, with how long ago they started.

### Node mix

How many nodes there are of each **instance type**, **capacity type** and **zone**, the most common first.

### Disruption

The node claims Karpenter is replacing, or could do without, with how long they've been that way:

| Status | Means |
| --- | --- |
| <span className="lumovi-status progressing">Terminating</span> | It's being deleted |
| <span className="lumovi-status progressing">Being replaced</span> | Karpenter tainted its node `karpenter.sh/disrupted`, to replace it |
| <span className="lumovi-status warning">Drifted</span> | It no longer matches its node pool or node class; Karpenter's message says what changed |
| <span className="lumovi-status neutral">Consolidatable</span> | Its pods fit elsewhere, or on a cheaper node |

When there are none, it says *Nothing is being disrupted.*

### Waiting for a node

The pods the scheduler couldn't place anywhere, which Karpenter launches nodes for, with the scheduler's message. When there are none: *No pods are waiting for a node.*

**Nodes** (for each node pool), **Disruption** and **Waiting for a node** show six rows, with **Show all** for the rest. Click anything to open it. On a cluster with thousands of nodes, **Nodes** shows as many as fit, its node pools in name order, and says so: *Shows 2,500 of 3,180: the node pools in name order, as many as fit. The tiles above count every node, and Lumovi’s Nodes list has them all.*

The overview reads node pools, node claims and nodes, and pods in every namespace, so it needs `list` on all four. A card whose list you can't read says why, and the others still show. Of nodes, its code is handed their names, labels, taints, capacity, conditions and usage; of pods, their names, namespaces, ages, nodes and conditions. It changes nothing.

## Every plugin on one page

**All plugins**, last under **Plugins** in the sidebar, lists every plugin Lumovi has: the ones it comes with, and the files in your folder. It opens by saying what a plugin is:

> A plugin teaches Lumovi a tool’s kinds: their columns, status, details and actions. Its actions change objects only with the patches and the objects they spell out, each shown here. A plugin of yours is data, and runs no code. The ones Lumovi comes with may also add pages drawn by their own code, in a sealed box: it reads only what its file says it may, and any change it proposes waits for you in Lumovi’s own dialog. A plugin has no access of its own: what it reads and changes, it does as you, and the cluster refuses what you may not do. Lumovi’s come inside Lumovi; yours are your own files.

On a server, it says "A plugin this server’s administrator added" in place of "A plugin of yours", and ends "this server’s are files its administrator put there." Where your organization's [policy](/desktop/policy#pluginsallowed) allows only some plugins, it adds: "Your organization’s policy allows only some plugins here: the others aren’t shown."

Each plugin is a row:

- **Plugin**: its name, its version and the kinds it shows (three, then how many more), with **Yours** on one from your folder.
- **On** the cluster: **On this cluster: in the sidebar** where the cluster serves one of its kinds and it has a place there, **On this cluster** for one with views only, or **Not on this cluster**. One of yours with the name of one of Lumovi's says **In use in place of Lumovi’s Flux**, and Lumovi's own row says **Not in use: your flux.yaml is used in its place, all of it.**
- **From**: **Lumovi**, which came with this Lumovi, or **Yours**, with the file's path.

**All**, **On this cluster**, **Yours** and **Not in use** narrow the list, and **Search plugins** finds one by its name or its file's. At the foot is where yours go, like *35 of 35 shown. Yours go in ~/.lumovi/plugins. One with the name of one of Lumovi’s is used in its place, all of it.*

### Newer plugins

In the desktop app, the page says which plugins have a newer version in the latest release of [Lumovi-plugins](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi-plugins), where Lumovi's come from. It looks when you open the page, and when you press **Check now**, and at no other time. A line above the list says what it found: *Lumovi-plugins release 1.3.0 · checked 2m ago*.

Lumovi takes a release only if it's signed by Lumovi-plugins' release workflow, shown to be built from its tag's commit, each file the one the release names, no older than one Lumovi has already taken, and not past the date its index says it expires. A release that fails one of these is used for nothing, and the line says which, like *Couldn’t check for newer plugins: the release isn’t signed by Lumovi-plugins’ release workflow, so Lumovi took nothing from it.* What the last good check found stays shown.

- **Updates** lists the plugins with a newer version, each with **Update:** and the version under its name, and **Update…**.
- A newer version that needs a newer Lumovi says so at the row's end, like *1.3.0 needs Lumovi 1.23.0*.
- **Not here yet** lists the release's plugins that this Lumovi doesn't have, **From** Lumovi-plugins, each with whether its kinds are on this cluster, and **Install…**. Open one to see what it would ask for, in the same words as any other.

These two filters show only when they'd list something.

The check asks only GitHub, for the release and its files, and sends nothing about you or your clusters. An organization's [policy](/desktop/policy#pluginstore) can turn it off (`pluginStore: off`), and so do `plugins: bundled` and `plugins: off`: the line then says "Your organization’s policy turns off checking for newer plugins." A server doesn't check.

### Install and update plugins

In the desktop app, **Install…** and **Update…** install a plugin from that release of Lumovi-plugins. Nothing installs or updates by itself.

Each opens a dialog first, **Install Argo CD 1.3.0?** or **Update Argo CD to 1.3.0?**, which says where it's from: "From Lumovi-plugins release 1.3.0, signed by its release workflow. What it reads and changes, it does as you." Then what the plugin may do, in the same lines as [What a plugin asks for](#what-a-plugin-asks-for):

- **An install** lists all of it, under **What it may do**.
- **An update** says which version is in use, then what's new first, under **New in 1.3.0**, with how many; then **No longer**, what it stops asking for; then whether its code changed, with **See what changed on GitHub**, which opens the changes between the two releases of Lumovi-plugins, and says under which folders this plugin's are. The rest is folded under **Everything else it may do, as before**. One that asks for nothing new says "It asks for nothing it doesn’t already have." What's new and what's gone are worked out from what each version asks, never from the words.

Nothing changes until you press **Install 1.3.0** or **Update to 1.3.0**. Then Lumovi checks the release again, as it did when it looked, and refuses if the plugin asks for anything but what you were shown, needs a newer Lumovi, or your organization's policy doesn't allow it. It writes the plugin's files whole, under the app's own folder, and checks them every time it reads them: a copy whose files aren't the ones you agreed to isn't used, and its row says "Its installed files aren’t the ones you agreed to, so Lumovi uses its own Argo CD." So does a copy that needs a newer Lumovi, or one your organization's policy no longer allows.

An installed plugin says so under **From**: *Installed from Lumovi-plugins release 1.3.0 · 1.2.0 kept to go back to*. Its code runs as the code of Lumovi's own does, in the same box, and **Run its code** turns it off. The count above the list says where they're from, like *32 come with Lumovi, 1 installed, 2 of yours*.

**More**, at the end of an installed plugin's row, goes back or removes it:

- **Back to 1.2.0**: the version before, kept on this computer. Only that one is kept.
- **Back to the one that came with Lumovi**: as built into this Lumovi.
- **Its release on GitHub**.
- **Remove Argo CD**, for one that doesn't come with Lumovi: "Its pages and lists go, and nothing in your cluster changes." It goes back under **Not here yet**.

Going back happens at once, unless the version you go back to asks for something the one in use doesn't. Then its dialog asks first, **Go back to Argo CD 1.2.0?**, and Lumovi holds it to what you were shown.

Where your organization's policy doesn't allow a plugin, its dialog says why and has only **Close**, like "Your organization’s policy doesn’t let plugins show what’s in Secrets, and this one would." and "Nothing is installed." (or, for an update, "1.2.0 stays in use."). A policy can allow only certain versions, as `pluginsAllowed: ["argo-cd@1.2.0"]`: see [pluginsAllowed](/desktop/policy#pluginsallowed).

Installing, updating, going back and removing are in the [audit log](/audit/events#settings-settings).

### What a plugin asks for

Open a row to see what the plugin asks for, read from its file in Lumovi's words. Nothing there is the plugin's own account of itself.

- **Run its code**, first, for a plugin with [pages of its own](#pages-drawn-by-a-plugin’s-code): its switch, and what its code does.
- **May change and create**: only the kinds it shows, by name, and the API groups they're all in; what its actions create, each one of its own kinds; and what no plugin can: what's the cluster's to set on an object (its owners, finalizers, name or namespace), and Kubernetes' own kinds. A plugin whose pages propose changes says which, like *Its pages may propose to change Applications, each change one you confirm in Lumovi.* A plugin without actions says **Nothing. It has no actions.**
- **May read**: its own kinds' objects, whose fields its columns, status and details show; the kinds of Kubernetes' it [shows](/reference/plugin-format#kubernetes’-own-kinds) and can't change; the kinds its related lists list beside an object, picked by their labels or fields; and the kinds it links to by name. For a plugin with code, what its code is handed of each kind of Kubernetes', field by field, like *Of Nodes, its code is handed only metadata.name, metadata.labels, spec.taints, status.allocatable and status.conditions, with their usage, as far as you may read them.* It ends with what it doesn't read, and that it sends nothing anywhere: "It reads no logs. It reads no Secret’s value. It sends nothing anywhere but to this cluster." A plugin whose pages show logs says instead "It shows containers’ logs: Lumovi reads them, and its code never sees them."
- **Adds to Lumovi's pages**: its pages, its tabs and on which kinds, and its commands in the palette, each by name. Where its pages don't run, it says why, like *Its pages don’t run here: Lumovi’s check of this browser didn’t pass.* Then which of Lumovi's own lists its pages may open, like *Its pages may open Lumovi’s lists of Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, Services, ConfigMaps, Ingresses, Jobs and CronJobs.*, and which of Lumovi's own places they may [link to](#links-to-lumovi’s-own-places).
- **Says what manages workloads**: where a view says [what it manages](/reference/plugin-format#what-it-manages), every object of those kinds in the cluster you may list (or, where you can't, in the namespaces you're looking at), to say which of them manages each workload, by labels and annotations the workload carries under the tool's own groups.
- **Its actions, and what each sends**: each action with the kinds it's on, whether it **asks first** or **runs at once**, and its patch as the file writes it, or the object it creates under **Creates**. What's filled in later is shown as what it is: `(now)`, `(what you choose)`, `(from the object)`, and `(removed)` for a field a patch takes away. An action's undo is shown under it as **Undo sends:**, and one that patches an object's status says so.

### When a file isn't in use

A file with a problem is a row too, by its file's name: **Not in use. 2 problems to fix in the file.**, with each problem under it, saying where in the file it is. Where the file has the name of one of Lumovi's plugins, the row adds **Lumovi’s Flux is shown instead**. **API resources** says only that there are problems, **A plugin couldn’t be used.** or how many, with **See Plugins** to come here. Every message is in the [plugin format](/reference/plugin-format#problems).

## Your own plugins

A plugin is one short YAML file, a document of kind `Plugin`, in `~/.lumovi/plugins`. This one gives two kinds of your own a place in the sidebar:

```yaml platform.yaml
apiVersion: lumovi.dev/v1beta1
kind: Plugin
metadata:
  name: platform
spec:
  version: 1.0.0
  needs:
    lumovi: 1.22.0
  groups: [platform.example.com]
  label: Platform
  icon: database
  kinds:
    - { group: platform.example.com, kind: Database }
    - { group: platform.example.com, kind: DatabaseBackup }
```

Press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>R</kbd>, and **Platform** appears under **Plugins**, with a tab for each kind.

- **`version`**, **`needs`** and **`groups`** say what it is: its own version, the oldest Lumovi it works with, and the API groups its kinds are in.
- **`kinds`** are the tabs, in order. `kind: '*'` with a `group` is every kind of that group, each with a tab.
- **`label`** is its name in the sidebar, and needed with `kinds`.
- **`icon`** is one of the [icons](/reference/plugin-format#icons).
- **`category`** puts it at the end of one of Kubernetes' sections instead: `cluster`, `network`, `config` or `storage`.
- **`groups`** hold every kind it names. A plugin is for a tool's kinds: Kubernetes' own can't be under its place, and a plugin can only [show](/reference/plugin-format#kubernetes’-own-kinds) the ones Lumovi has no page for.

A place in the sidebar only groups kinds. How each kind looks, its columns, status and actions, comes from a view, which the plugin holds under `views:`. See [Write a plugin](/custom-resources/write-a-plugin).

### Replacing one of Lumovi's

A kind is one plugin's, so a plugin of yours can't show a kind one of Lumovi's has, unless it takes that plugin's place. Give yours the same `metadata.name` as Lumovi's, like `flux` or `cert-manager` ([Included plugins](/custom-resources/included-plugins) gives each one's name). Yours is then used in its place, all of it: Lumovi's place in the sidebar and every one of its views go.

```yaml flux.yaml
apiVersion: lumovi.dev/v1beta1
kind: Plugin
metadata:
  name: flux
spec:
  version: 1.0.0
  needs:
    lumovi: 1.22.0
  groups: [kustomize.toolkit.fluxcd.io, helm.toolkit.fluxcd.io]
  label: GitOps
  icon: git-branch
  kinds:
    - { group: kustomize.toolkit.fluxcd.io, kind: Kustomization }
    - { group: helm.toolkit.fluxcd.io, kind: HelmRelease }
```

This one has no views, so its two kinds get the API server's columns, and Flux's other kinds are no longer in the sidebar. To change a little, start from all of it: copy the file from [Lumovi's plugins](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/tree/main/plugins) and edit the copy. **API resources** names your file under **Shown by**, and **All plugins** says on its row that it's in use in place of Lumovi's.

Under any other name, a plugin of yours that shows one of those kinds isn't used, and the problem says what to do: "certificates.yaml: Certificate.cert-manager.io is cert-manager’s kind. A plugin of yours named cert-manager takes its place, all of it".

### When something's wrong with it

A plugin is used whole or not at all. If anything in its file is wrong, none of it is used, and [All plugins](#when-a-file-isn’t-in-use) lists everything wrong with it under the file's own row. When that leaves one of Lumovi's in use in place of yours, the row says so: "Lumovi’s Flux is shown instead". Every field, and every message, is in the [plugin format](/reference/plugin-format).

<Note>
  **In your cluster:** plugins everyone sees go in the chart's `plugins` value. People can't add their own from a browser. See [Plugins for everyone](/server/plugins).
</Note>
