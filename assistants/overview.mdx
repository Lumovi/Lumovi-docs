Lumovi is an MCP server, so any AI assistant or agent that speaks MCP (any MCP client) can use it to look at your clusters: list what's there, read objects, events and logs, and find what's wrong, with Lumovi's own reasons. When an assistant wants to change something, it asks. Lumovi shows you the change, with the diff it makes, the assistant's reason and the `kubectl` command that does the same, and makes it only if you approve.

## On your desktop, or your team's server

Assistants use the desktop app on your computer, or your team's Lumovi [in a cluster](/server/overview) or [fleet](/clusters/fleet). They get the same tools either way, keep to the same [permissions](/assistants/permissions), and their changes wait for you in the same dialog. What differs:

| | Desktop app | Your team's Lumovi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where they connect | `http://127.0.0.1:47830/mcp`, on your computer only | The server's address, like `https://lumovi.example.com/mcp` |
| How they sign in | With a token of Lumovi's, copied into their settings | In your browser, through Lumovi: you sign in, and allow each one |
| Which assistants | Any MCP client: over HTTP, with Lumovi's token, or one Lumovi starts for it over stdio | Any MCP client that connects over HTTP and signs in with OAuth |
| As whom | You, with your kubeconfig | You, signed in as on your pages |
| On or off | Off until you turn it on | On unless the administrator turned it off |
| What they may do, and where | [Your permissions](/assistants/permissions) | Your permissions, within the administrator's limits |
| Where you approve them | Lumovi's window, with a notification from your computer | Your Lumovi pages, with the browser's notification if you allow it |

How it works, and what assistants can do, below, is the same on both. Turning them on and connecting them is the desktop app's; for your team's Lumovi, see [AI assistants on a server](/assistants/server).

## How it works

Lumovi has no AI of its own, and needs no API key. The AI is your assistant's, with your account there. Lumovi is a tool it uses: it speaks the [Model Context Protocol](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) (MCP), which assistants use to connect to tools, and gives them [twelve tools](/assistants/tools): seven that read, four that ask for a change, and one that waits for your answer. The assistant decides when to call them, and Lumovi answers with what it would show you.

Lumovi works for an assistant as it does for you:

- **Your clusters.** Every context in your kubeconfig, as on the start screen; on a server, the clusters you may see there.
- **Your credentials.** Lumovi reads and changes clusters with the credentials in your kubeconfig, or on a server, the ones you signed in with, so an assistant can't see or do more than your account can. See [Permissions](/clusters/permissions#ai-assistants).
- **Your guard rails.** [Read-only](/changes/read-only) clusters stay read-only, and your [permissions](/assistants/permissions) say what assistants may see, change and read, and where.

## What assistants can do

**They read.** Your clusters, the kinds each one serves, lists of objects with their health, one object as YAML, events and a container's logs, as your [permissions](/assistants/permissions) say. One tool, `find_problems`, sums up what's failing in a cluster or namespace in one answer, which is where assistants usually start.

**They ask.** To apply a manifest, scale, restart or delete, each time with a reason. The cluster checks the change first, with a dry run, and then it waits in Lumovi for you to approve or reject it. Once you approve it, Lumovi makes it only if it still does what you saw. Your permissions can let them make some changes without asking, or none at all, in some clusters and namespaces, but deletions always ask: see [Changes they ask for](/assistants/approvals#changes-they-ask-for).

What they can't do:

- **See what your permissions hide.** By default, a Secret's keys but not its values, and env values that look sensitive, read `(hidden by Lumovi)`. A namespace you hide isn't there for them.
- **Change a read-only cluster**, or where your permissions say **Never**.
- **Go past your RBAC.** A change your account can't make fails at the dry run, before you're asked.
- **Delete without asking.** Deletions always wait for you, and some ask you to type the name first.
- **Anything else.** They have only Lumovi's tools: no shells, port forwards or Helm.

## Turn it on

AI assistants are off until you turn them on. Open the **AI assistants** page, on its **Connect** tab:

- Choose **AI assistants**, the sparkles at the bottom of the sidebar. It has a green dot while an assistant is connected.
- On the start screen, choose the same button at the bottom, next to the theme.
- Choose **AI assistants…** in the [command palette](/explore/finding-things) (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> on Windows and Linux).
- Choose **View → AI Assistants…**

The page's **Back** button takes you back where you were. Its other tabs are [Permissions](/assistants/permissions), and **Activity**: what assistants changed, or asked to, since Lumovi opened, each with its `kubectl` command. Its link, **Everything they did, kept, is in the audit log.**, opens the [audit log](/audit/overview) with what assistants did: every change, with your answer, and every tool they called.

While it's off, **On this computer** says what assistants would do:

- **They read:** Clusters' objects, events and logs, and what's failing, as your AI permissions say.
- **They ask:** Each change shows here, with the diff it makes, for you to approve or reject.
- **Only on this computer:** With a token of its own. Read-only clusters, and your own access (RBAC), always apply.

Choose **Turn on**. Lumovi starts listening for assistants, makes a token for them to send, and shows where:

```text
Listening at http://127.0.0.1:47830/mcp
```

It stays on: Lumovi listens again each time it starts. The switch at the top right of **On this computer** turns it off. Lumovi then stops listening, lets every assistant go, and withdraws every change still waiting. Turned on again, it keeps the same token.

Your organization's [policy](/desktop/policy) can turn AI assistants off on its computers. Then the switch is disabled, and instead of **Turn on**, the tab says "Your organization's policy turns AI assistants off on this computer." Assistants that were connected can't connect while it does.

### The port

Lumovi listens on port 47830 unless you choose another. To change it, type a port from 1024 to 65535 in **Port**, and choose **Use**, or press <kbd>↵</kbd>.

If another program already uses the port, the tab says so, in red, and shows no way to connect until you choose another:

> Lumovi can't use port 47830 (listen EADDRINUSE: …): choose another one.

Changing the port lets every assistant go and withdraws every change still waiting. Assistants you set up with the old port need setting up again. One that starts Lumovi through its bridge, as Claude Desktop does, doesn't: it finds the port by itself.

## Connect an assistant

Under **Connect an assistant**, a tab says how for a few assistants, step by step, and **Other** for any other MCP client. What you copy has the token in it: the tab shows only its first four characters, then `…`, and the copy buttons copy all of it. The assistant keeps the token in its own settings.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Claude Code" icon="square-terminal">
    Run the command once, in a terminal. **Copy command** copies it, with the whole token:

    ```bash
    claude mcp add --transport http --scope user lumovi http://127.0.0.1:47830/mcp --header "Authorization: Bearer <token>"
    ```

    With `--scope user`, Claude Code uses Lumovi in every project, not only the folder you run it in.

    The tab suggests letting Claude Code use Lumovi's tools without asking you each time: Lumovi asks you before any change anyway.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Claude Desktop" icon="monitor">
    Choose **Add to Claude Desktop**, then quit Claude Desktop and open it again:

    > Added to Claude Desktop: quit and open it again to use Lumovi there.

    Lumovi adds itself to Claude Desktop's settings, as `lumovi` under `mcpServers`, and leaves the rest of the file as it was:

    | System | Claude Desktop's settings |
    | --- | --- |
    | macOS | `~/Library/Application Support/Claude/claude_desktop_config.json` |
    | Windows | `%APPDATA%\Claude\claude_desktop_config.json` |

    Claude Desktop doesn't connect over HTTP. It starts Lumovi as a program it talks to over stdin and stdout. On macOS, that's Lumovi's app with `--mcp-stdio=` and Lumovi's settings folder. On Windows, where the app itself can't read stdin, it's `Lumovi.exe` running as Node (`ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE`) on a small bridge that comes with Lumovi. Started either way, Lumovi opens no window: it passes Claude Desktop's messages on to the Lumovi you have open. It reads the port and the token from Lumovi's settings each time, so a new port or token needs no setting up again. Before each message, it makes sure it's Lumovi that listens on that port, with a challenge only Lumovi can answer, and only then sends the token: see [Security](#security).

    The entry it adds looks like this, with your own paths. To write it by hand, copy it as Lumovi would: on Windows, the `env` line is needed.

    <CodeGroup>
      ```json macOS
      "lumovi": {
        "command": "/Applications/Lumovi.app/Contents/MacOS/Lumovi",
        "args": ["--mcp-stdio=/Users/you/Library/Application Support/Lumovi"]
      }
      ```

      ```json Windows
      "lumovi": {
        "command": "C:\\Users\\you\\AppData\\Local\\Programs\\Lumovi\\Lumovi.exe",
        "args": [
          "C:\\Users\\you\\AppData\\Local\\Programs\\Lumovi\\resources\\app.asar.unpacked\\out\\mcp-stdio\\bridge.cjs",
          "C:\\Users\\you\\AppData\\Roaming\\Lumovi"
        ],
        "env": { "ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE": "1" }
      }
      ```
    </CodeGroup>

    If Lumovi isn't open, it's opened for Claude Desktop, the way you last started it, and Claude Desktop's messages wait up to 30 seconds for it to listen. AI assistants have to be on in Lumovi for this.

    Lumovi writes Claude Desktop's settings whole, through a copy it then puts in their place, so they're never left half-written. Choosing **Add to Claude Desktop** again updates Lumovi's entry. Moved Lumovi? Add it to Claude Desktop again: the entry names where Lumovi's app was.

    Claude Desktop isn't made for Linux, and there the tab says so: connect other assistants as **Other** shows.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Cursor" icon="mouse-pointer-2">
    Choose **Add to Cursor**. Cursor opens, and asks you to add Lumovi.

    Or add Lumovi to `~/.cursor/mcp.json` yourself. **Copy Cursor's settings** copies it, with the whole token:

    ```json ~/.cursor/mcp.json
    {
      "mcpServers": {
        "lumovi": {
          "url": "http://127.0.0.1:47830/mcp",
          "headers": {
            "Authorization": "Bearer <token>"
          }
        }
      }
    }
    ```

    If the file has other servers already, add `lumovi` next to them, under `mcpServers`.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="VS Code" icon="code">
    Choose **Add to VS Code**. VS Code opens, asks you to confirm, then shows Lumovi among its MCP servers.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Other" icon="plug">
    Any other MCP client, or AI agent, that can connect to an MCP server over HTTP uses Lumovi's address, and sends its token in a header. **Copy address** and **Copy header** copy them:

    - **Address**: `http://127.0.0.1:47830/mcp`
    - **Header**: `Authorization: Bearer <token>`

    Lumovi speaks MCP's Streamable HTTP transport there. Keep `127.0.0.1`, as Lumovi writes it, not `localhost`: see [Security](#security).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Connected now

**Connected now** lists the assistants connected at the moment, each by the name it gives as it connects, or a shorter one Lumovi knows it by (Claude Desktop's is **Claude**). Two of the same show once, with how many, like **Claude Code ×2**. Until one connects, it says "None yet: assistants show here once they connect."

The sidebar's button says so too: its green dot, and **AI assistants (2 connected)** when you point at it.

An assistant leaves the list when it disconnects. One that hasn't asked anything for six hours is let go: the next time it asks, Lumovi tells it to start a new session.

## A new token

Assistants send Lumovi's token to connect. If a copy of it got out, make a new one: choose **New token…** at the bottom of the **Connect** tab, then **Make a new token**. **Cancel** keeps the one you have.

Lumovi says what that does: "Assistants connected with the token now disconnect: set them up again (Claude Desktop picks the new one up itself)." Every connected assistant is let go, every change still waiting is withdrawn, and the old token stops working. Set up again each assistant you gave the token to, from the **Connect** tab. Claude Desktop, through Lumovi's bridge, reads the new token by itself.

## Security

Lumovi lets assistants in only from this computer, and only with its token:

- **Only on this computer.** Lumovi listens on `127.0.0.1`, which other computers can't reach, and writes its address that way. Assistants that connect over HTTP send the token to that address with no challenge, so keep `127.0.0.1` where you set one up by hand: `localhost` can lead to `::1` first, where another program could be listening, and be given the token.
- **Only with the token.** Every request has to carry it, as `Authorization: Bearer <token>`. It's 43 random characters, made the first time you turn assistants on, and Lumovi compares it in constant time. A request without it gets "Lumovi needs its token: set this assistant up again from Lumovi."
- **Not from web pages.** A browser marks the requests a page makes with an `Origin` header, and Lumovi refuses them: "Web pages may not connect to Lumovi." A page that points a name of its own at your computer (DNS rebinding) sends that name as the `Host`, and is refused too: "Only this computer may connect." Only requests addressed to `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`, at Lumovi's port, get in.
- **The stdio bridge checks it's Lumovi first.** An assistant that starts Lumovi as a program, as Claude Desktop does, talks to it through a bridge. Before every request, it sends a random challenge (`Lumovi-Challenge`), and Lumovi answers with the challenge's HMAC-SHA256, keyed with its token (`Lumovi-Proof`). Only Lumovi can make that, and it tells nothing of the token. Without the right answer, the bridge sends no token, starts no Lumovi, and the assistant is told: "What listens on Lumovi's port (47830) can't show it's Lumovi, so Lumovi's token isn't sent to it: open Lumovi, and set this assistant up again."
- **Small messages.** A message is at most 4 MB. A larger one gets "That's more than Lumovi takes: 4 MB at most." Only `/mcp` answers.
- **Kept where only you can read it.** The token is in Lumovi's `settings.json`, with what assistants may do. On macOS and Linux, Lumovi keeps that file readable by your account only. See [Privacy and security](/reference/privacy-and-security#what-it-keeps-on-your-computer).

Any program on your computer that has the token can use Lumovi as an assistant would: read your clusters, and ask for changes. The commands and settings you copy have it in them, and a command you ran in your shell's history too. Make a new token if one got out.

Lumovi itself calls no AI service, and sends what it gives an assistant to that assistant only. The assistant handles it as it handles everything else you show it, which for most means sending it to their AI provider. Your [permissions](/assistants/permissions) say what Lumovi hides from them: by default, Secrets' values and env values that look sensitive. A ConfigMap's data, or what a container logs, they read unless you hide its namespace, or its logs. See [What's hidden](/assistants/tools#what’s-hidden).
