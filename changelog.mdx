Lumovi follows [semantic versioning](https://semver.org): a fix is a patch release, anything new a minor one, and a change that breaks how people use it a major one. Every release, with its installers and checksums, is on [GitHub](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/releases). The full record is in [CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md).

<Update label="October 7, 2026" description="1.9.1" tags={["Desktop app"]}>
  ## Security fixes

  What an AI assistant's bridge, a cluster or a kubectl mirror can do is narrower.

  ### Fixed

  - **Claude Desktop's bridge proves it's talking to Lumovi** before it sends Lumovi's token. While Lumovi wasn't running, another program on the computer could listen on its port and be given the token. Now, before every request, the bridge sends a challenge that only what holds the token can answer, a keyed hash of it, and it doesn't start Lumovi over something else. See [Security](/assistants/overview#security).
  - **What a cluster says can't write over a terminal's first line.** Lumovi's own lines leave out control characters and the marks that reorder text, and cut short what they quote from a cluster or a mirror.
  - **No kubectl older than 1.25**, Lumovi's oldest. A cluster can't choose how old a kubectl a terminal gets: older clusters' terminals use yours, saying why.
  - **kubectl mirrors over HTTPS only**, or on this computer: over plain http, the checksum comes from the same place, and both could be changed on the way. That goes for an organization's policy and for `LUMOVI_KUBECTL_MIRROR`. An empty `LUMOVI_KUBECTL_MIRROR` is as good as none.
  - **Each cluster's version is remembered across restarts**, so a terminal for a cluster that can't be reached as Lumovi starts gets the kubectl kept for it. A download a crash cut short is cleaned up as Lumovi starts.

  [Release notes](https://github.com/Lumovi/Lumovi/releases/tag/v1.9.1)
</Update>

  ## Tools of its own

  The desktop app brings what its Helm actions and terminals need.

  - **A kubectl that matches the cluster, in each terminal.** The kubectl for the version the cluster says it runs: the newest patch of that minor version, as kubectl works a minor version either side of its cluster. It's downloaded from dl.k8s.io once, checked against the checksum published beside it, and kept for every cluster on that version. It's first on the terminal's `PATH`, even after your startup files (zsh, bash, sh, dash, ksh, fish and PowerShell), and the terminal's first line says which: "It's v1.34.9, to match the cluster."
    - **A terminal never waits long for it.** A cluster that doesn't say its version within 3 seconds, or a kubectl still downloading after 10, and the terminal starts with yours, saying why; the terminals opened after get it. Offline, the newest one kept does.
    - **View → Match kubectl to Each Cluster** turns it off. An organization's [policy](/desktop/policy#kubectl) can too (`"kubectl": false`), or name a mirror of dl.k8s.io to get it from, as can `LUMOVI_KUBECTL_MIRROR`.
    - See [A kubectl that matches the cluster](/debug/terminal#a-kubectl-that-matches-the-cluster).
  - **helm, shipped with the app**, and listed in its bill of materials. Helm 4.3.0, the same as the image's, runs its Helm actions: installing, upgrading, rolling back and uninstalling releases need no helm of your own. `LUMOVI_HELM` still names another. Terminals have it last on their `PATH`, for when you have none. See [Which helm runs](/helm/upgrade-and-rollback#which-helm-runs).

<Update label="October 7, 2026" description="1.8.0" tags={["In your cluster", "Desktop app"]}>
  ## A restart signs nobody out

  Upgrading, or restarting, your team's Lumovi keeps everyone signed in, and the AI assistants they allowed connected.

  - **What's kept:** sessions, the AI assistants people allowed with their tokens, and what each person made read-only for their assistants.
  - **Where it's kept:**
    - With the chart, in a Secret it makes, `<release>-state`, which Lumovi may only `get` and `patch` (`auth.keepSessions`, on by default).
    - Without the chart, in that Secret (`LUMOVI_STATE_SECRET`), or under `LUMOVI_DATA_DIR`.
  - **Sealed, with a key kept apart.** Each entry is sealed (AES-256-GCM), and filed under an HMAC of what it is. The key is in a Secret of its own: the chart makes `<release>-state-key` and keeps it, or you give `auth.stateKeySecret` where the chart is rendered with `helm template`, as with Argo CD. Without Kubernetes, the key is in `state.key`, beside `state.json`. Reading the state shows nothing, not even who's signed in, and writing it can't make, change or move an entry that opens.
  - **No credential in it either.** Cookies and assistants' tokens are kept by their hash. A token a session passes on to the cluster (its person's own, with a token, or with single sign-on that forwards theirs) is sealed again, with a key only its cookie gives. It's unsealed when its browser comes back, and renewed from then on.
  - **Sign-outs stay signed out.** A sign-out, and an assistant let go or signed out, is kept before Lumovi says it's done. When that can't be kept, Lumovi says so.
  - **Assistants after a restart.** Until its person opens Lumovi again, an assistant whose session passes their own token on says Lumovi restarted, and to open it. It carries on once they do. Assistants behind a proxy, or whose sessions pass no token on, carry on at once.
  - See [Across restarts](/server/security#across-restarts).

<Update label="October 7, 2026" description="1.7.0" tags={["Desktop app", "In your cluster"]}>
  ## Behind a company's proxy

  Lumovi's own connections go through a company's proxy, and trust its certificate authorities, as `kubectl` and `helm` do.

  - **The proxy.** Lumovi uses `HTTPS_PROXY` and `HTTP_PROXY` (`http://` where they give no scheme) for clusters, single sign-on, Helm repositories, the audit webhook and a fleet agent's hub. It skips what `NO_PROXY` leaves out: names and what's under them, and addresses, each with a port or for any, the same for all of them (not ranges). A cluster's `proxy-url` in its kubeconfig is used where it has one. A proxy that wants credentials (407), or won't open a tunnel, says so. Its credentials are never said, nor is a proxy that isn't a URL.
  - **The desktop app** takes the proxy from your login shell, as it takes `PATH`. A server leaves out the cluster's API server and services.
  - **Certificate authorities.** The operating system's are trusted besides Node's: that's where IT installs the one an HTTPS-inspecting proxy signs with. On a server, so are those in the files `LUMOVI_CA_FILE` names, which Helm is given too. A certificate Lumovi doesn't trust, an expired one, or one for another name says which, and what usually causes it behind such a proxy. A file of them that can't be read says which.
  - **In the chart:**
    - `proxy.https`, `proxy.http` and `proxy.noProxy`, or `proxy.secret` for a proxy with credentials: a Secret with `HTTPS_PROXY`, and `HTTP_PROXY` if plain http goes through one;
    - `extraCA`: a ConfigMap's or a Secret's certificate authorities;
    - OpenShift's restricted SCC (`restricted-v2`), which chooses the pod's user, group and fsGroup (`openshift: auto`).
  - See [Proxies and certificate authorities](/server/network), and on your computer, [Proxies and certificates](/desktop/network).

  ## A policy for your organization's computers

  A policy sets what the desktop app does on every computer. It's deployed by an MDM, Group Policy, Intune or configuration management, where only an administrator can write it:

  - on macOS, `/Library/Application Support/Lumovi/policy.json`, root's, as is its folder;
  - on Linux, `/etc/lumovi/policy.json`, root's, as is its folder;
  - on Windows, the registry's `HKEY_LOCAL_MACHINE\SOFTWARE\Policies\Lumovi`, its `Policy` value.

  What a policy can set:

  - **Read-only**: every cluster, or those whose names match.
  - **AI assistants**: off, or limited by rules like a server's `LUMOVI_ASSISTANT_RULES`.
  - **Updates**: Lumovi's own turned off, for IT to deploy new versions.
  - **The network**: the proxy and certificate authorities to use.

  What it sets is locked ("Set by your organization"). A person's own settings are back when it's gone. A policy that can't be used (not an administrator's, not JSON, a key twice, a key Lumovi doesn't know, a registry Lumovi can't read) locks the most it could: every cluster read-only and assistants off, saying why. What the desktop app couldn't set up as it started (its policy, a certificate authority's file, a proxy) is said on the page, too. The policy sets what Lumovi does. What people may do in a cluster is still its RBAC's to say. See [Policy for your organization's computers](/desktop/policy).

  ## Checkable releases

  - **Bills of materials.** Each installer comes with an attested CycloneDX bill of materials (`Lumovi-<version>.cdx.json`): every package the app bundles or ships, and Electron. The image carries its JavaScript's, attested with it, and its system's.
  - **Signed with cosign**, keyless, by the release workflow from `main`: the image and the chart, both as a Sigstore bundle and with the `.sig` tag older admission controllers look for. See [Check a release](/server/security#check-a-release).
  - **A hardening guide** for a production installation: sign-in, the network, the service account, Access, the audit log, and an image pinned by digest whose signature an admission controller requires. See [Harden it for production](/server/hardening).
  - **Report a vulnerability** by email too: security@lumovi.dev.

  ### Fixed

  - When the desktop app starts, it waits for the login shell's environment before asking anything of a cluster. So nothing is sent before it knows the proxy, or a credential plugin's `PATH`.

<Update label="October 6, 2026" description="1.6.0" tags={["Desktop app", "In your cluster"]}>
  ## An audit log

  What was done through Lumovi, by whom, from where, and how it went.

  - **Recorded as it happens.** Changes from the page and from AI assistants, with how each was approved: by whom, after how long, with your note, or made without asking. Helm installs, upgrades, rollbacks and uninstalls. Shells and node shells, with how long they ran, and port forwards. Logs, Secrets and Helm releases' values read, or refused. On a server, sign-ins and sign-outs (failed ones too, up to 60 a minute, then counted), and assistants allowed, denied and let go. Each tool an assistant calls. Changes to read-only, where node shells run, and AI permissions. Each change comes with the `kubectl` or `helm` command that does the same. Never Secrets' values, what a change set, Helm values, tokens or manifests, nor what the cluster says in an error about a Secret, and each event is one line of 64 KiB at most. See [The audit log](/audit/overview).
  - **The Audit log page**, from the sidebar, the command palette, the Activity log and the AI assistants' Activity tab. Search it, filter it with the filters kept in the address, follow events as they happen, see everything about one, and export to CSV or JSON Lines. Each object's **Audit** tab shows its own.
  - **Chained.** Each event holds its chain's id and the hash of the one before it. **Check integrity** lists every place the chain doesn't hold, and anyone can check it with jq and sha256sum (`jq -jcS 'del(.hash)' | sha256sum`). The newest's hash, compared with a copy kept elsewhere, shows what the chain alone can't: the newest removed, or all of it rewritten. See [Check it hasn't changed](/audit/verify).
  - **In your cluster:**
    - People see their own events, and the auditors you name in `audit.auditors` (`LUMOVI_AUDITORS`) see everyone's.
    - Each event is also a JSON line on Lumovi's output, and can go to a webhook (`audit.webhook`), in batches, tried again until it's taken. What can't be kept or sent is counted, said in the log, recorded, and shown to auditors.
    - The history is kept on a 2 GiB volume, from the cluster's default storage class, which uninstalling keeps, by one Lumovi at a time. A cluster without a default class needs `audit.persistence.storageClass`, or `audit.persistence.enabled: false`, which keeps it in memory.
    - `audit.level: changes` records less.
    - See [Audit log for your team](/server/audit-log).
  - **The desktop app** keeps its own on your computer, for 90 days, or in memory, saying why, if its folder can't be used.

  ## Who may do what, team by team

  On your team's Lumovi, its admins decide what people may do through it: within their RBAC, and never more.

  - **Profiles, grants and limits.** What everyone signed in gets. Lumovi's own groups, made of your identity provider's groups and people by name, whatever the case of their name. Profiles, each a level of changes, shells, node shells, logs, Secrets (hidden, keys only, values), Helm (upgrade, or install), AI assistants (off, changes ask first, as they set them) and the audit log (their own, or everyone's). Grants that give groups a profile in some clusters and namespaces, by name, pattern or label. And limits that hold anyone back wherever they match. See [Access for your team](/server/access).
  - **The server decides.** Changes, shells, node shells, logs, Secrets, Helm releases and AI assistants are refused where people may not, saying which grant or limit decided. Changes, shells, logs, Secret reads and Helm refused this way are recorded in the audit log as refused. Secrets' values never leave the server for someone shown only their keys, not even in a change's answer, nor do Helm releases' values and manifests.
  - **The Access pages**, for the admins named in `access.admins` (`LUMOVI_ADMINS`):
    - groups, with who's in each, and the provider's groups seen at sign-in (named, for Microsoft Entra ID's IDs);
    - profiles side by side;
    - grants and limits, with how far each reaches;
    - someone checked anywhere;
    - and every change, from the audit log.

    Changes are read back and saved together. Saving after someone else did is refused, never lost in theirs.
  - **Your access**, for everyone: what they may do, cluster by cluster, and why. Every refusal says why where it happens, and links to it. See [Your access](/clusters/your-access).
  - **In the chart**: `access.policy` (`LUMOVI_ACCESS`), which admins see but can't change, with `on` and `off` taken as meant, quoted or not. What admins set is kept in a ConfigMap the chart makes, and read again every 15 seconds, so replicas follow. A change made there by hand is recorded too, as made outside Lumovi, even one made while Lumovi wasn't running. With no admins, everyone may do all their RBAC allows.
  - **AI assistants** never do more than their person may. Someone who may use them nowhere can't allow one.

  ### Fixed

  - Saving AI rules on a server keeps the chart's `helm.sh/resource-policy: keep` on their ConfigMap, so turning AI assistants off with `helm upgrade` keeps everyone's rules.
  - AI rules changed just as someone else's save landed are no longer lost.

<Update label="October 6, 2026" description="1.5.0" tags={["Desktop app", "In your cluster"]}>
  ## What AI assistants may do, and where

  You decide what your AI assistants see, change and read: everywhere, and differently in some clusters and namespaces.

  - **A page of their own.** **AI assistants** opens a page, from the sidebar, the command palette or **View**, in place of its dialog: **Connect**, **Your assistants** (on a server), **Permissions**, and **Activity**, what they changed or asked to. See [AI assistants](/assistants/overview).
  - **Five settings.** Visibility, Changes (**Without asking**, **Ask you**, **Never**), Secrets (**Values**, **Keys only**, **Hidden**), Env values (**Show**, **Hide sensitive**, **Hide all**) and Logs (**Read**, **Don't read**). By default, changes ask, Secrets show their keys only, and env values that look sensitive are hidden: by a word in their name, like `DB_PASSWORD` or `apiKey`, or a value that carries credentials, like a URL with a password or a token, or a webhook's address. See [What assistants may do](/assistants/permissions).
  - **Rules for some clusters and namespaces**, by name, pattern (`tenant-*`) or label (`team=payments`), or leaving one out (`!kube-system`). Where they overlap, the strictest wins. A namespace a rule hides isn't there for assistants, and every refusal says which rule decided. A change to a cluster's own objects (nodes, CustomResourceDefinitions, cluster roles, webhooks…) can reach every namespace, so it's decided as the strictest of them all: not made where one is hidden, refused or hides its Secrets.
  - **Checked as you go.** The tab counts what assistants see, change and read in every namespace, checks any one and says why, and shows what assistants are told as they start. Changes apply at once, to connected assistants too.
  - **Without asking, carefully.** Deletions, changes that take fields over, and changes that make a workload read a Secret it didn't before still ask you, unless assistants may read Secrets' values there.
  - **On a server**, each person's are kept on the server, the same in every browser: in a ConfigMap the Helm chart makes, a file under `LUMOVI_DATA_DIR`, or memory. Administrators set limits nobody's own loosen, with `assistants.rules` (`LUMOVI_ASSISTANT_RULES`), matching a cluster of its own by the labels in `clusterLabels` (`LUMOVI_CLUSTER_LABELS`). **Allow an AI assistant?** says what it will be able to do. See [AI assistants for your team](/server/assistants).
  - **Changed: `assistants.changes` is a limit now.** `ask` and `never` cap what people choose, and `allow` leaves it to them. The chart no longer sets it, so after upgrading, people may let their assistants change things without asking, or read Secrets' values. To keep 1.4's limits, add `{ name: Limits, changes: ask, secrets: keys }` under `assistants.rules`: see [Upgrading](/server/upgrade). In the desktop app, each cluster's Ask, Allow or Never becomes a rule, and works as before.

  ## AI assistants on your team's Lumovi

  Claude Code, Cursor, VS Code and other MCP clients connect to a Lumovi server, or a fleet, at its address, and act as whoever allows them.

  - **Signed in through Lumovi.** Add `https://lumovi.example.com/mcp` to the assistant, and it opens Lumovi in the browser: you sign in as you always do, with a token, single sign-on or your company's login, and allow it. No token to copy. See [AI assistants on a server](/assistants/server).
  - **As you, until you say.** It reads the clusters you can see, a fleet's too, with your RBAC on each, and changes nothing in the clusters you made read-only. It lasts until you disconnect it in Lumovi, sign out, or your session ends; behind a proxy, as long as a session lasts.
  - **Your answer, on your pages.** The changes it asks for wait on your own Lumovi pages, in the same approval dialog as on the desktop. **Notify me** lets the browser tell you while the tab is in the background, and with no page open, a change waits for the next one.
  - **On after upgrading, with someone's say.** Assistants are on unless you turn them off (`assistants.enabled`, `LUMOVI_ASSISTANTS`), and each one needs someone to sign in and allow it. Say what their changes do, for every cluster or each one (`assistants.changes` and `assistants.clusters`, or `LUMOVI_ASSISTANT_CHANGES=ask,staging=allow,production=never`); deletions always ask. The server's log records each assistant allowed or let go, and what became of every change. See [AI assistants for your team](/server/assistants).
  - **Sent back only where they may be.** After someone allows one, Lumovi sends them back only to their own computer, an app, VS Code's site, or sites you name (`assistants.redirectHosts`, `LUMOVI_ASSISTANT_REDIRECT_HOSTS`). A refresh token that comes back after it was used ends what was allowed.
  - **One replica.** What assistants are allowed lives in Lumovi's memory, so the chart keeps one replica while they're on, and refuses more. Below a base path, its ingress routes the two addresses at the origin's root where assistants look for how to sign in. Behind an authenticating proxy, let the assistants' paths through: see [Authenticating proxy](/server/auth/proxy#ai-assistants).

  ### Fixed

  - **The desktop app's bridge** for assistants that start Lumovi themselves, like Claude Desktop, connects only to Lumovi on this computer. A port in Lumovi's settings that named another host would have sent it the bridge's token.

<Update label="October 6, 2026" description="1.3.0" tags={["Desktop app", "In your cluster"]}>
  ## Your AI assistant, with you in charge

  Assistants read your clusters through Lumovi, and the changes they ask for wait for your approval. And right-sizing for clusters of thousands of namespaces.

  ### AI assistants

  Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code and other assistants that speak MCP can read your clusters through the desktop app, and ask you before they change anything.

  - **They read** with Lumovi's tools: your clusters, the kinds each one serves, lists with each object's health, one object as YAML, events and logs, and what's wrong in a cluster or namespace in one answer. A Secret's keys, never its values. See [Assistant tools](/assistants/tools).
  - **They ask.** Applying a manifest, scaling, restarting and deleting are checked by the cluster with a dry run first, then wait in Lumovi with the assistant's reason, the diff, whose fields they'd take over (Helm's, Argo CD's…) and the `kubectl` command, for you to approve, or reject with a note the assistant reads. A notification says when one's waiting; unanswered, it ends after five minutes, and the assistant keeps waiting for your answer with `wait_for_change`. See [Approving changes](/assistants/approvals).
  - **Made as you saw it.** Once you approve a change, Lumovi tries it again, and makes it only if it still does what the diff showed; a deletion is only ever of the object you saw. Approving the deletion of a namespace, a node or another object that's hard to get back, or anything in a cluster named like production, asks for its name, as Lumovi's own Delete does.
  - **Each cluster decides** whether its assistants' changes are asked about, made without asking (deletions, and changes that take fields over from others, still ask), or never made. Read-only clusters and your own RBAC always apply. The Activity log shows each change, and which assistant asked.
  - **Set up in a click.** **AI assistants**, at the bottom of the sidebar, in the command palette or under **View**, turns it on and adds Lumovi to Claude Desktop, Cursor and VS Code, or gives Claude Code a command to paste. Claude Desktop starts Lumovi when it isn't running.
  - **Only on this computer.** Lumovi listens on `127.0.0.1`, for assistants with its token, kept in settings only you can read on macOS and Linux, and refuses web pages. It calls no AI service, and needs no API key. See [AI assistants](/assistants/overview).

  ### Changed

  - **Right-sizing on big clusters.** It asks Prometheus about many namespaces at once, up to a thousand containers' worth, biggest first, instead of one namespace at a time: a cluster of thousands of namespaces takes dozens of queries. A batch Prometheus finds too big is asked about in halves. What's measured shows while the rest is, and the workloads come 50 a page. See [Big clusters](/metrics/right-sizing#big-clusters).

<Update label="October 5, 2026" description="1.2.0" tags={["Desktop app", "In your cluster"]}>
  ## Hands on: shells on nodes, and terminals

  A shell on any node, as root, through a pod Lumovi starts and deletes again. And on the desktop, terminals on your computer, with kubectl already pointed at the cluster you're looking at.

  ### Shells on nodes

  - **A node's Shell tab** starts a privileged pod on it and opens a shell on the node itself, as root in its own namespaces, or in the pod, with the node's files under `/host`. The pod is deleted when the shell ends. See [Shells on nodes](/debug/node-shell).
  - **It says what it will do**, before anything starts, shows each step as it happens, and says what went wrong in words that say what to do: a namespace whose Pod Security doesn't allow privileged pods, an image the node can't pull or without `nsenter`, a node with no shell of its own (Talos, Bottlerocket).
  - **Your RBAC decides**, as with `kubectl debug node`. Where the pods run and their image are settings for each cluster. In your cluster, the chart's `nodeShell` values set them for everyone, or turn node shells off. See [Helm values](/server/helm-values).

  ### Terminals on this computer

  - **A dock of terminals**, in the desktop app, along the bottom of every cluster's pages. Open it from the **Terminal** bar, with <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>&#96;</kbd>, **View → Terminal**, or **New terminal** in the command palette, and another with <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>&#96;</kbd>. See [Terminals](/debug/terminal).
  - **Your own shell, pointed at the cluster.** Each tab is your shell, with kubectl, helm and the rest pointed at a cluster and namespace, in that terminal only. A kubeconfig of Lumovi's comes first in `KUBECONFIG` and only names them, so your credentials stay in your files.
  - **A terminal's keys.** Tabs can be renamed. In a terminal on macOS, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> (or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>N</kbd>) opens another, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> closes one, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> clears it, and <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> and <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> move between them; on Windows and Linux, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>T</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>PgUp</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>PgDn</kbd>. See [Keyboard shortcuts](/reference/keyboard-shortcuts#terminals).

  ## Fleet: many clusters in one Lumovi

  One Lumovi can now show your team many clusters in a browser, private ones too. It runs in a cluster, or without Kubernetes, on a machine of its own or a platform like Sevalla. This release also fixes five things, on the desktop and in your cluster.

  ### Fleet

  - **Every cluster on one page.** Lumovi's home page sums up each cluster as its overview would: nodes, pods, workloads, warnings, CPU and memory, its version and how fast it answers. What needs attention comes first, and a cluster that can't be reached says why. Filter by status and labels, group by a label, and find a workload by name or namespace in every cluster. The cluster switcher and the command palette move between clusters, and back to all of them. See [Clusters in a fleet](/clusters/fleet).
  - **Each person's own RBAC.** People sign in once, with single sign-on or through a proxy. Lumovi impersonates them in each cluster, or passes their own token on to clusters that trust your identity provider. See [A fleet of clusters](/server/fleet).
  - **Where clusters come from.** A kubeconfig, in a setting (`LUMOVI_FLEET_KUBECONFIG`, as YAML or base64 on one line) or in files read again as they change; [Secrets](/server/fleet/discovery) of the cluster Lumovi runs in, its own, Cluster API's and Argo CD's; and that cluster itself. A context's `lumovi.dev` extension, or a Secret's annotations, give a cluster labels, the groups who see it, and whether people's own tokens are passed on.
  - **[Agents](/server/fleet/agents) for private clusters.** For a cluster Lumovi can't reach, an agent in it dials out to Lumovi over a WebSocket, and relays its connections to the API server. TLS runs from Lumovi to the API server through it, so the agent passes on only what it can't read. It reconnects by itself, also when its connection goes quiet, and passes on its service account's new token when Kubernetes rotates it.
  - **The Helm chart** installs a fleet (its `fleet` values), what a cluster needs to [join one](/server/fleet/members) (`mode: member`: a service account that may only impersonate, and its token, with the kubeconfig entry printed after installing), or an agent (`mode: agent`).
  - **Without Kubernetes.** On platforms that set `PORT`, like [Sevalla](/server/fleet/sevalla), Lumovi listens on it unless `LUMOVI_PORT` is set, and takes every setting from the environment.
  - **To try it from source**, `npm run dev:server -- --fleet` serves a fleet of the demo clusters, signed in to with a mock single sign-on. See [Build from source](/reference/build-from-source).

  ### Fixed

  - **Views from the Helm chart are used.** Kubernetes mounts the chart's `views` value as links, which Lumovi left out. A view file that can't be read is now listed as a [problem](/reference/view-format#problems), and the rest load.
  - **The [Map](/explore/map) shows other namespaces' gateways and services.** A Gateway or a Service in another namespace that a route refers to, like a shared gateway, no longer says <span className="lumovi-status critical">Not found</span>. It's shown without a status, and opens.
  - **[Right-sizing](/metrics/right-sizing) loads without VerticalPodAutoscalers or nodes.** When the Vertical Pod Autoscaler is installed but your account can't list its autoscalers, the page loads, and says what that means. It also loads for accounts that can't list nodes.
  - **<kbd>Esc</kbd> closes a Helm release's panel**, as its close button says. See [Helm releases](/helm/releases).
  - **The distribution on [Usage history](/metrics/usage-history) counts in the singular** when it should: "1 namespace", not "1 namespaces".

  ## The first version of Lumovi

  Lumovi is the Kubernetes dashboard that was called KubeStacks. This first version under its new name has everything KubeStacks had. Coming from KubeStacks? See the [FAQ](/reference/faq) for what doesn't carry over.

  ### Two ways to run it

  - **On your desktop.** An app for macOS, Windows and Linux that opens every cluster in your kubeconfig, with client certificates, tokens and credential plugins found on your login shell's `PATH`. It updates itself: new versions download in the background and install when you restart or quit. **Help → Check for Updates…** looks right away.
  - **In your cluster.** A container image, `ghcr.io/lumovi/lumovi` for `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64`, and a Helm chart serve the same app as a dashboard your team opens in a browser. People sign in with a token the cluster accepts, with single sign-on (OpenID Connect), or through an authenticating proxy, and see and change what their own RBAC allows. Every page has an address you can share.

  ```bash
  helm install lumovi oci://ghcr.io/lumovi/charts/lumovi \
    --namespace lumovi --create-namespace
  ```

  Get started with [Run it in your cluster](/get-started/in-your-cluster).

  ### Seeing what's going on

  - **Overview** with node, pod, workload and warning health, CPU and memory against capacity, and what needs attention.
  - **Lists for every kind**, with health, sorting, filters and label selectors. Big lists load in chunks and stop at 5,000 objects, so huge clusters stay fast.
  - **Workloads** of every kind in one list, pods that nothing manages included, with status, ready pods, autoscaler ranges and usage.
  - **A detail panel** next to the list, with kind-specific facts, containers, conditions, events, logs and YAML. Secret values stay hidden until revealed.
  - **[Map](/explore/map)**, a tab on every object: what leads to it, from gateways and ingresses to services, and what it owns, uses and runs on, down to the nodes. What's missing, and what nothing uses, stands out.
  - **Logs** streamed as they're written, one pod's or every pod's of a workload merged by time, with search and filters.
  - **Usage history** from Prometheus or VictoriaMetrics, found for you: a Metrics page that ranks namespaces, workloads, pods and nodes, and a Metrics tab on pods, workloads and nodes.
  - **[Right-sizing](/metrics/right-sizing)**, a tab of Metrics: what each Deployment, StatefulSet and DaemonSet should request, from a week of its usage, and why. A recommendation is applied once the API server has checked it, with undo.
  - **Helm releases**, read from where Helm 3 and 4 keep them, with values, the objects they made, notes and diffs between revisions.

  ### Custom resources, views and add-ons

  - **Every kind the cluster serves**, found through API discovery, with the columns `kubectl get` prints and a status read from their conditions.
  - **Views** describe a kind's columns, status, details, links, related objects and actions as data. Lumovi ships its own, and yours in `~/.lumovi/views` add to them or replace them.
  - **[Add-ons](/custom-resources/add-ons)** give each tool the cluster runs an entry in the sidebar, with everything of its kinds in one list, failing first. Lumovi has add-ons for 32 tools, from Argo CD, cert-manager and Flux to Istio, KEDA and Velero.
  - **Karpenter's add-on** opens on an overview: node pools against their limits, the nodes they launched, what's being disrupted and why, and the pods waiting for a node.

  ### Changing things

  - **Actions** to scale, restart, change images, roll back, pause and resume rollouts, run CronJobs, suspend and resume, cordon, uncordon and drain, evict and force-delete, change autoscaler ranges, expand volumes, and edit labels, annotations and YAML.
  - **Shells and debug containers**, and port forwards on the desktop.
  - **Helm upgrades and installs**, reviewed as a server-side dry run first, and **rollbacks and uninstalls**, all with your own `helm`.
  - **Bulk actions** with progress per object and a retry for failures, and **create from YAML** with templates, checked by the cluster before anything is created.
  - **Guard rails**: permission checks, the `kubectl` command in every dialog, typed confirmation for risky deletes, undo, an activity log and read-only mode.

  ### Around the app

  - **A command palette**, keyboard shortcuts, a native menu with the same commands, and light and dark themes.
  - **Calm errors**: a banner when a cluster stops responding, the last data kept on screen, and error pages with details to copy or report.
