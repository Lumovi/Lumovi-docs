**Search** finds objects of every kind you may list, in the namespaces picked, by part of their name or by their labels. The [command palette](/explore/finding-things#the-command-palette) goes to a page or an object you have in mind; Search is for "where is everything called `checkout`?" across Deployments, Services, ConfigMaps and the rest at once.

Open it from **Search** in the sidebar, after **Helm releases**, or with <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> on Windows and Linux, where a [terminal](/debug/terminal) keeps it while it has the keyboard). In the desktop app, it's also **Go → Search the Cluster…**. In the palette, type, then press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>↵</kbd> from any row, or pick **Search the cluster for "…"**, its last row. Pressed again on Search, the key selects what's in its field.

## What you can type

| Type | To find |
| --- | --- |
| `checkout` | Any part of a name, in any case |
| `app=checkout` | A label with this value |
| `tier!=db` | Not this value, or no such label |
| `team=*` | Has this label, any value |
| `!canary` | Doesn't have this label |
| `checkout app=web` | Both: every term must match |

Words are parts of a name, and every word must be in it. A term that isn't a label as Kubernetes writes one is a word. Before you type, Search shows these, and your saved searches.

## What it finds

What matches is grouped by kind, in the sidebar's order, then the cluster's own kinds by name. In each group, a name that's exactly what you typed comes first, then names that start with it, then the rest, by name. A group shows 50 rows, then **Show all**.

A row has the object's name, with what you typed marked, its namespace and its age. A label search shows the labels it matched. Otherwise, a pod shows its restarts, and a workload how many of its pods are ready. Kinds that have a status, like pods and deployments, show it, as their lists do. Click a row to open the object.

The line above the groups says how many objects matched, in how many kinds. When nothing does, Search says so, like "No object's name has "checkout"."

## What it reads

Search reads each kind's names, namespaces, labels and ages, never what its objects hold. So Secrets and ConfigMaps are found by name and label alone, never by a value. On a server, Secrets aren't found in a namespace where [your access](/clusters/your-access) hides them.

- **Once.** Each kind is read when Search opens, when the namespaces change, and on **Refresh**. Typing matches against what was read, and reads nothing more. What's shown isn't kept up to date: once it's over a minute old, the line at the bottom says how old, with **Refresh**.
- **In the namespaces picked.** Kinds outside namespaces, like Nodes, are searched only with **All namespaces**.
- **Kinds you may list.** One your access doesn't let you list is named at the bottom as not searched. Events aren't searched: find them on the [Events](/explore/events) page, and on an object's own.
- **A few at a time.** Each kind has 10 seconds to answer. One that doesn't says "didn't answer in time", and one that fails "couldn't be read", each with **Retry**. **Stop** lets go of the kinds not read yet, and **Search them** reads them after all.
- **Up to the list limit.** A kind is read up to 5,000 objects (`LUMOVI_MAX_LIST_ITEMS`), as a [list](/explore/finding-things#label-selector) is. Past that, its group says "Searched the first 5,000 of 12,000", and that the rest weren't searched by name: choose fewer namespaces, or add a label. A label search asks such a kind again, for just those labels, so the cluster sends only the objects that have them, wherever they are in the kind.

## In the command palette

Once you've typed two characters, the palette's **Objects** group shows the objects whose names match, as Search finds them: up to eight, best first, across every kind, each with its status. Pages, views and actions that match come before them, so <kbd>↵</kbd> does what it did. <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>↵</kbd> searches the cluster for what you typed.

## Saved searches

**Save this search** keeps what you typed. Up to 50 can be saved, each up to 200 characters. They're yours in every cluster, run in the cluster you're in and its namespaces:

- **On Search**, under the field, and before you type.
- **In the palette**, with nothing typed, under **Saved searches**.
- **With a key.** The first nine run with <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>9</kbd>: <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd> on Linux, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd> on Windows, where <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd> switches the keyboard's language. Not while you type in a field, with a dialog or menu open, on a phone, or in a browser on Linux, which keeps those keys for itself, as it does [pins'](/explore/finding-things#pin-an-object).

**Saved**, pressed again, removes it, as does the **×** beside it; **Undo** puts it back. Removing one moves those after it up a key, and Lumovi says so, like "Saved search 3 is now ⌥⇧2."

In the desktop app, they're kept with your [settings](/explore/settings), on this computer. On a server, they're kept with who's signed in, where it keeps [pins and views](/explore/finding-things#where-pins-and-views-are-kept).
