The sidebar's **Configuration** and **Storage** sections hold five lists:

| List | Shortcut | Columns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ConfigMaps** | <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> | Keys |
| **Secrets** | <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>X</kbd> | Type, Keys, Expires |
| **Resource Quotas** | From the sidebar or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> | Fullest |
| **Limit Ranges** | From the sidebar or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> | Types |
| **Volume Claims** | <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd> | Capacity, Storage class, Volume |
| **Volumes** | From the sidebar or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> | Capacity, Claim, Storage class, Reclaim |
| **Storage Classes** | From the sidebar or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> | Default, Provisioner, Reclaim, Binding |

Volumes and storage classes aren't in a namespace, so their lists always show all of them. (<kbd>⌘</kbd> is <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> on Windows and Linux.)

When the cluster has them, the sections also hold [plugins](/custom-resources/plugins): **Sealed Secrets** under Configuration, and **Longhorn**, **Rook Ceph** and **Volume Snapshots** under Storage.

Every one of these objects also has a **Map** tab: what uses a ConfigMap, Secret or claim, and what a claim is bound to, down to its volume and storage class. See [Map](/explore/map).

## ConfigMaps

Open a ConfigMap and its **Data** section shows each key with its value, as text. Binary values (`binaryData`) are counted in the **Keys** column, and are in the YAML.

## Secrets

Secret values stay hidden until you ask for them.

  <img className="hidden dark:block" loading="lazy" src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/Lumovi/Lumovi@main/docs/screenshots/secret-dark-1x.webp" alt="A secret's details next to the Secrets list: three keys with their sizes and hidden values, and Reveal all." />
</Frame>

- **The list** shows each secret's type and how many keys it has. Lumovi drops the values before the list reaches the page.
- **Data** shows each key with its size, and its value as `••••••••`. Reveal one key with its eye button, or all of them with **Reveal all**. Values are decoded from base64 for you.
- **Copy** next to a key copies its value without revealing it, for pasting somewhere safe.
- **On a Lumovi server**, your [access](/clusters/your-access) may show you a namespace's Secrets' keys only: **Data** says "Only the keys", each value reads **Value hidden by your access**, and **YAML** says **Values hidden by your access**. Their values never reach your browser, not even in what a change answers with, and editing a Secret as YAML is off, since that writes it whole. Where your access hides Secrets, you can't open or change them, and lists across every namespace leave them out.
- **The [audit log](/audit/overview)** records who opened a Secret, with its keys, never its values. Opening one counts, revealed or not: its values reach the page, hidden.
- **YAML** hides values too, until you choose **Reveal values**. The `kubectl.kubernetes.io/last-applied-configuration` annotation holds a copy of the values, so it's hidden as well. Under **Annotations** in **Overview**, it reads *Hidden: it holds the secret's values*.

### Certificates

Where you may see a Secret's values, Lumovi says what the certificates in it say: a TLS Secret's `tls.crt`, and any other value that holds a PEM certificate, like a `ca.crt`. The certificates are read on the server, or in the desktop app's own process, and only what's below reaches the page. A private key is never read.

- **In its panel**, **Certificate** comes before **Data**, with each key's: its **Subject**; the **Names** it's for, DNS names and then IP addresses; its **Issuer**, or *Self-signed*; when it's **Valid**, from and to; and its **Chain**, how many certificates follow it, and the one of them that ends first. A certificate that has expired, isn't valid yet, or ends within 14 days says so, *Expired*, *Not yet valid* or *Expires soon*, and so does the panel's top. A bundle of certificates that aren't one chain, like a set of roots, is only counted. A value Lumovi can't read as a certificate says so.
- **The list** has an **Expires** column: how long until the first of a Secret's certificates ends, its chain's included, like *in 87d*, or how long ago. **Not valid** and **Expires soon**, above the list, show only those Secrets.
- **Where your access shows a Secret's keys only**, or hides it, none of this shows, and nothing of it is read for you.
- **The [audit log](/audit/events#access-access)** records it as reading the Secret: "Read the certificates of Secret storefront-tls", and, for a list, "Read the certificates of 14 Secrets in shop".
- **If the cluster doesn't answer**, the panel says "Lumovi couldn't read the certificate just now. It's trying again.", and tries every 5 seconds for a minute or so. Then it offers **Try again**.

A value is read for its first 64 KB and its first 8 certificates, and a certificate shows its first 50 names, with how many more there are.

### Editing a secret

When you edit a Secret's YAML, its text values appear decoded, under `stringData`, so you can read and change them as text. Values that aren't text stay base64-encoded under `data`. On save, everything is encoded back into `data`. See [Edit YAML](/changes/yaml).

## Resource quotas and limit ranges

A quota's row shows its fullest resource, with a meter and how full, like "93% · 3.75 Gi of 4 Gi". It's <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Within quota</span>, <span className="lumovi-status warning">Nearly full</span> from 90%, <span className="lumovi-status critical">Full</span> at its limit, each with an icon as well as a colour, or *Not counted yet* before the cluster has counted it. Its **Overview** has a meter for each resource it limits; a resource it forbids reads "Not allowed · 0 of 0", and a quota with a scope says whose pods it counts, like "Every pod in shop".

A limit range says what it sets, a line for each kind of thing: "A container's default request 100m CPU, 64Mi memory", "A pod up to 4 CPU, 8Gi memory", "A volume claim from 1Gi to 100Gi".

A namespace's **Overview** shows its quotas and limit ranges, a quota from 90% is in **Needs attention**, and a Deployment whose pods a quota keeps from being made says so, with the quota to open: "1 of its 3 pods can't be made: the namespace's quota compute has no room for its memory request.", and **Open quota compute**.

A claim is <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Bound</span> when it has a volume, and shows its phase otherwise, like <span className="lumovi-status warning">Pending</span>. Open one for its capacity and the size it asked for, its access and volume modes, and links to its storage class and volume.

**Expand volume…** grows a bound claim. Pick the new size in Mi, Gi or Ti. It starts at about twice the current size, and must be larger than it is now: volumes can't shrink. If the claim's storage class doesn't allow expansion, Lumovi says so instead of trying. See [Actions by kind](/changes/actions).

## Volumes

A volume is <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Bound</span> or <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Available</span> when all is well, and shows its phase otherwise, like <span className="lumovi-status warning">Released</span>. Open one for its capacity, access modes, reclaim policy, storage class, and a link to the claim that holds it.

## Storage classes

Open a storage class for whether it's the **Default**, its provisioner, reclaim policy and binding mode, whether **Volume expansion** is allowed, and its parameters.

<Warning>
  Deleting a volume claim, a volume or a storage class can lose data, so Lumovi asks you to type its name first. See [Changing things safely](/changes/safely).
</Warning>
