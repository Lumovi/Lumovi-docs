The sidebar's **Network** section has three lists. Like every list, they follow the [namespace](/clusters/namespaces) you picked.

| List | Shortcut | Columns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Services** | <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd>, or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>4</kbd> in the desktop app | Type, Cluster IP, External, Ports |
| **Ingresses** | <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>I</kbd> | Class, Hosts, Address |
| **Network Policies** | From the sidebar or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> | Pod selector, Policy types |

On Windows and Linux, use <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> where these pages say <kbd>⌘</kbd>.

These kinds have no health of their own, so their lists sort by name. To see whether a service has pods behind it, open it.

Open a service to see:

- **Details**: its type, cluster IP, external address, session affinity and selector.
- **Ports**: each port's name, port, target, node port and protocol.
- **Pods**: the pods its selector matches, failing ones first. If this list is empty, the service has nowhere to send traffic.
- **Logs**: the logs of every pod behind it, merged in the order they were written. Handy for following a request across replicas. See [Logs](/debug/logs).
- **Events** and **YAML**.

### Reach a service from your computer

<Badge size="sm" color="gray" stroke icon="monitor">Desktop app</Badge>

**Forward a port…** on a service listens on a port on your computer and passes connections to the service's first ready pod, like `kubectl port-forward service/web 8080:80`. It works for services with a selector. See [Port forwarding](/debug/port-forwarding).

## Ingresses

Open an ingress for its class, address and TLS hosts, and its **Rules**: each host and path, and the service and port it sends traffic to (`web:80`).

Using the Gateway API or Istio instead? Their kinds, like `HTTPRoute`, `Gateway` and `VirtualService`, have views with the columns and links that matter for them. See [Built-in views](/custom-resources/built-in-views).

## Network policies

Open a policy for the pods it applies to (its **Pod selector**, or *All pods in namespace*), its policy types, and how many ingress and egress rules it has. The rules themselves are in **YAML**.
