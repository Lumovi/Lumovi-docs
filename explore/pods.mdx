**Pods** lists every pod in the namespace you picked, failing ones first. Open it from the sidebar, with <kbd>G</kbd> then <kbd>P</kbd>, or with <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>3</kbd> in the desktop app (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>3</kbd> on Windows and Linux).

## The list

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Status** | What the pod is doing, in words: <span className="lumovi-status healthy">Running</span>, <span className="lumovi-status critical">CrashLoopBackOff</span>, <span className="lumovi-status warning">Unschedulable</span>, <span className="lumovi-status progressing">ContainerCreating</span>… |
| **Ready** | Ready containers, of all of them |
| **Restarts** | How many times its containers restarted |
| **CPU**, **Memory** | Live usage, from [metrics-server](/metrics/live-usage) |
| **Node** | The node it runs on |
| **Age** | How long ago it was created |

The status is the most specific reason Lumovi can find: a container's waiting reason (like `ImagePullBackOff`), why a pod failed (like `OOMKilled` or `Evicted`), or why it can't be scheduled. Hover it for the message behind it. [Health and status](/explore/health) lists them all.

## Open a pod

Click a row, or move to it with <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> and press <kbd>↵</kbd>.

**Overview** starts with the facts: the node it runs on, its pod and host IPs, QoS class, service account, restart policy, and **Controlled by**, a link to the ReplicaSet, Job or other object that manages it.

Then a card for each container, init containers first:

- Its image and ports.
- Its state: *Running for 2h*, *Running for 5m, not ready*, the reason it's waiting (hover for the message), or how it ended, like *Error (exit code 1)*.
- How many times it restarted.
- Its CPU and memory use right now, next to its requests and limits.
- **Last terminated**, when it has restarted: the reason and exit code of its previous run. This is how an `OOMKilled` restart shows on a pod that's running again.

After the containers come the pod's conditions, labels and annotations.

### The other tabs

| Tab | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| **Logs** | The pod's logs as they're written, with search and filters, and its previous run's. See [Logs](/debug/logs). |
| **Shell** | A terminal in any running container. See [Shells and debug containers](/debug/shell). |
| **Metrics** | Its CPU and memory over time, against requests and limits. See [Usage history](/metrics/usage-history). |
| **Events** | What happened to it, like failed image pulls and probe failures. |
| **YAML** | The whole object, to read, copy or edit. See [Edit YAML](/changes/yaml). |

## What you can do

From the panel, the **⋯** menu (<kbd>.</kbd>), the row's right-click menu or the command palette:

- **Shell** and **Debug…**, to look inside. A debug container works even in images with no shell.
- **Forward a port…**, to reach the pod from your computer. <Badge size="sm" color="gray" stroke icon="monitor">Desktop app</Badge>
- **Restart**, for a pod something manages: it's deleted and its controller makes a new one.
- **Evict…**, through the Eviction API, so PodDisruptionBudgets are respected.
- **Delete…**, with **Force** for a pod stuck terminating.

Each one is described in [Actions by kind](/changes/actions). Right-click a pod for **Copy kubectl logs** too, alongside **Copy name** and **Copy kubectl describe**.

## Thousands of pods

Lists load in chunks of 500 and only draw the rows on screen, so a cluster with thousands of pods scrolls as smoothly as one with ten. Long lists refresh less often, and a list stops at 5,000 objects with a note saying so. Use a label selector to see the rest, or raise the limit with `LUMOVI_MAX_LIST_ITEMS`.
