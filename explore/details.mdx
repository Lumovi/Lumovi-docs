Click any row, or move to it with <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> and press <kbd>↵</kbd>, and its details open in a panel next to the list. The list stays where it was, so you can keep comparing. While the panel is open, moving through the list with the arrow keys opens each row in turn.

## The header

At the top: the object's kind, namespace and age, its name (with a button to copy it), and its status. Below them are up to two of the actions you'll use most for that kind, like **Scale**, **Restart**, **Shell** or **Cordon** (or **Edit YAML**, for kinds without one), and **⋯** for the rest. Press <kbd>.</kbd> to open that menu from the keyboard. (<kbd>⌘</kbd> is <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> on Windows and Linux.)

| Control | Shortcut |
| --- | --- |
| **More actions** (**⋯**) | <kbd>.</kbd> |
| Delete | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>⌫</kbd> |
| **Keep open below** / **Show below** (desktop app) | |
| **Expand panel** / **Restore panel** | |
| **Close** | <kbd>Esc</kbd> |

Drag the panel's edge to make it wider or narrower, or focus the edge and use <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd>. It starts 600 pixels wide, and remembers the width you choose.

## Tabs

Each kind gets the tabs that make sense for it:

| Tab | Shown for |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Everything |
| **Pods** | Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, ReplicaSets, Jobs, services with a selector, nodes, custom kinds that scale pods (like Argo Rollouts), and custom kinds whose [view](/custom-resources/overview#views) relates pods to them. A view can name the tab, like CloudNativePG's **Instances**. |
| **Logs** | Pods, and the same kinds as **Pods** except nodes. A workload's or service's logs merge every pod's. |
| **Shell** | Pods, and nodes: a shell on the node itself, through a pod Lumovi starts there. See [Shells on nodes](/debug/node-shell). |
| **Metrics** | Pods, nodes, Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, ReplicaSets, Jobs and CronJobs, and custom kinds with pods |
| A view's lists | Custom kinds whose view relates other objects to them: a tab for each, like a node pool's **Nodes** or a Kafka cluster's **Topics** |
| **Map** | Everything but events and namespaces |
| **Events** | Everything but events |
| **Audit** | Everything |
| **YAML** | Everything |

Where the panel is too narrow for them all, the tabs that fit stay in their order, and the rest are under **More**, at the row's end; on a phone, it opens a sheet. The tab that's open always has a place in the row. From the keyboard, **More** comes after the last tab: <kbd>→</kbd> past it, or <kbd>End</kbd>, then <kbd>↓</kbd>, <kbd>↵</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> opens it.

### Overview

**Details** comes first: when the object was created, **Controlled by** (a link to its owner, like the ReplicaSet behind a pod), and the facts that matter for its kind. A Deployment shows its replicas, strategy and selector; a Service its type, IPs and selector; a node its OS, kernel, runtime and kubelet.

Then a section for what's particular to the kind:

| Kind | Section |
| --- | --- |
| Pods | **Containers**: each one's image, state, restarts, usage against requests and limits, and its last termination |
| Nodes | **Capacity** and **Taints** |
| Workloads | **Pod template**: each container's image |
| Services | **Ports** |
| Ingresses | **Rules**: host, path and backend |
| ConfigMaps, Secrets | **Data**, with Secret values hidden until revealed |

Then **Conditions**, **Labels** and **Annotations**, when it has them. Conditions show at a glance which are healthy, with their reason, message and when they changed.

Names of related objects (a pod's node, a claim's volume, an owner) are links: click one to open it.

### Custom resources and other kinds

For kinds without a page of their own, the Overview shows the object's **Spec**, **Status** and other fields as a tree. Short values are inline, nested ones are indented, and deep ones start folded.

Hover a field's name to read what it means. The explanation comes from the kind's OpenAPI schema, which clusters serve from Kubernetes 1.27. If a [view](/custom-resources/overview) covers the kind, its facts go in **Details**, and the objects it links to are in **Related**. Lists a view relates to the object, like a node pool's nodes, get tabs of their own.

### Pods

The pods the object runs or selects (or, for a node, every pod on it), with their status, ready containers and restarts, failing ones first. Click one to open it.

### Map

How the object is connected: above it, what leads to it, like the ingress, service and autoscaler in front of a Deployment; below it, what it owns, uses and runs on, down to its pods' nodes. Missing references stand out, and clicking a card opens that object's own map. See [An object's map](/explore/map#an-object’s-map).

### Events

The events about this object, newest first. Often the quickest answer to "why won't it start?"

### Audit

Who changed the object through Lumovi, read it, or opened a shell in it, and how it went, from the [audit log](/audit/overview): its 50 latest events, newest first, and new ones as they happen. On a server, you see your own, unless you're one of its auditors. **Open in the audit log** shows them all, as far back as the log keeps. See [An object's Audit tab](/audit/overview#an-object’s-audit-tab).

### YAML

The whole object, syntax highlighted. **Copy YAML** copies it, and **Edit** opens it for [editing](/changes/yaml), checked by the cluster and shown as a diff before it's saved. For a Secret, values stay hidden until you choose **Reveal values**, and **Hide values** hides them again.

## Keep it open below

In the desktop app, **Keep open below**, beside **Expand panel** (or in the [command palette](/explore/finding-things) with the object open), keeps the object open below the pages, as a tab beside the [terminals](/debug/terminal), on the tab it was showing. A pod's logs keep coming, and a Deployment's metrics keep moving, while you go elsewhere, other clusters included. Once it's kept, the button says **Show below**, and shows its tab.

Its tab in the bar has the kind's icon, its name (cut in the middle when it's long), the tab it shows unless that's **Overview**, and its cluster when that isn't the window's. It's read in its own cluster, as you, as the panel would read it. Right-click it for **Open in the panel**, **Move to a new window**, **Close** and **Close the others**; **Open in the panel** (↗) is also at the top of what it shows. With the focus below, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> on Windows and Linux) closes the tab shown.

- **Deleted**, its tab says *deleted*, and what it last showed stays, dimmed: "Pod cart-7d9f6c5b8-x2kqp was deleted at 14:32. Its lines up to then stay here until you close this tab." (On another tab than Logs: "Its last state stays here…") Where Lumovi knows what ran it and that's still there, it says so, "It belonged to the Deployment cart, which is still there.", with **Keep cart open instead**. **Close this tab** lets it go.
- **Unreachable**, when its cluster stops answering, its tab says *unreachable*, and a strip says "Can't reach staging since 14:30. Lumovi tries again on its own, and the lines go on from the last one here." (On another tab: "…what's here is from 14:30.") Once the cluster answers, it's as before.

**Move to a new window** opens it alone in a smaller window, over the one it came from, under a band that names it with its tab, namespace and cluster. One on its **Shell** moves on its overview, since another window would start another shell. **Put back below**, in the band, returns it below in the window it came from, else in the one in front on its cluster, else the one in front; with no other Lumovi window open, its own becomes one, with it below. Closing the window lets it go.

Each window keeps its own, and they come back when Lumovi starts again: see [Opened again as it was](/get-started/desktop#opened-again-as-it-was). One on a cluster that signs in through a program waits there as *not read yet* until you ask. A server's web page and a phone have no tabs below, so nothing is kept there.

## Compare with another

**Compare with…**, in **More actions** and the [command palette](/explore/finding-things#the-command-palette), sets the object beside another of its kind: in another namespace, or another cluster. Pick a cluster, then a namespace, then the object; one with the same name is marked **Same name**. A cluster that signs in through a program says "Signs in when opened", and is read only when you choose **Open** on its row.

The page shows what differs, each side as its YAML, with **Swap** to change sides. "No differences" says so.

- **Left out until you ask**: the object's status, and what the cluster set itself: uids, versions, times, managed fields, owners, kubectl's and controllers' annotations, and what it was given, like a Service's address or a pod's node. **Show status and cluster-set fields** puts them back, and Lumovi remembers the choice.
- **A workload's config**: the ConfigMaps and Secrets each side refers to are compared too, paired by where they're referred to (a volume, an env var, an image pull secret) rather than by name, which often differs between where they run.
- **Secrets**: values are masked on both sides until **Reveal values**, and a changed value shows as a changed line with neither value. Where your access on either side shows a Secret's keys only, only keys are compared, and values say *not compared*.
- **Withheld fields**: a field a plugin's file [withholds](/reference/plugin-format#param-withholds) reads *(withheld)* on both sides, never as a difference.

Each side is read with your own access on its cluster, as opening it would be, and recorded in the [audit log](/audit/overview) the same way. Where a side can't be read, the page says why, and nothing is compared until it is.

## Right-click a row

Every row has a menu with the object's actions, and:

- **Open**
- **Copy name** and, for objects in a namespace, **Copy namespace/name**
- **Copy kubectl describe**, a ready-to-run command for this object
- **Copy kubectl logs**, for pods

## Links to an open object

The open object is part of the address, so **Back** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd>) and **Forward** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd>) move between objects you opened, the way a browser does.

<Note>
  **In your cluster:** every page, and every open object, has an address you can share. Send a teammate the link to a failing pod, and it opens on the same pod for them, with their own permissions.
</Note>
