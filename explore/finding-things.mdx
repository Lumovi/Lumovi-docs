Two habits cover most of it: press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> to go anywhere, and press <kbd>/</kbd> to filter the list you're on. On Windows and Linux, use <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> instead of <kbd>⌘</kbd>.

## The command palette

Press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> (or click **Search…** in the header) and type. Case doesn't matter. Views and commands match when every word you type appears in them, in any order, so `vol cl` finds Volume Claims. Objects match when their name contains what you typed.

Results come in groups:

| Group | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Actions** | When an object is open: everything you can do to it. Actions you can't take are shown, disabled. |
| **Objects** | Once you've typed two characters: objects whose names match, among those Lumovi has already loaded in this cluster (up to 20). |
| **Go to** | Every page: the overview, Workloads, Metrics and [Right-sizing](/metrics/right-sizing), [Alerts](/explore/alerts), Helm releases, API resources, the page of each [plugin](/custom-resources/plugins) the cluster has (marked **Plugin**), and every kind the cluster serves, found by name, kind, short name or API group. Custom resources included. |
| **Namespace** | **All namespaces**, and every namespace. |
| **Clusters** | Every cluster in your kubeconfig, and **All clusters**. When Lumovi runs in your cluster, only in a [fleet](/clusters/fleet): every cluster you may see, and **All clusters**, the fleet's page of clusters. |
| **Help & appearance** | **Create…**, **New terminal** and **New window** (in the desktop app), **Metrics source…**, **AI assistants…**, **Audit log**, **Access** (for Lumovi's admins, on a server), **Your access** (on a server), **Keyboard shortcuts**, **Use system theme**, **Use light theme**, **Use dark theme**, and **Make** _cluster_ **read-only** or **Allow changes to** _cluster_. The last one isn't there when `LUMOVI_READ_ONLY` makes every cluster read-only. |

Move with <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd>, press <kbd>↵</kbd> to pick, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close.

**New terminal** opens a [terminal](/debug/terminal) in the dock at the bottom of the page: your own shell, with kubectl pointed at the cluster you're in and its namespace. The palette shows its key, <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>&#96;</kbd>, which works anywhere in a cluster. Typing `kubectl` or `shell` finds it too.

**AI assistants…** opens the **AI assistants** page: connect an [assistant](/assistants/overview) (turning them on, in the desktop app), see the ones you allowed on a [server](/assistants/server), say [what they may do](/assistants/permissions), and see what they did. Typing `mcp` finds it too.

**Audit log** opens the [audit log](/audit/overview): what was done through Lumovi, by whom, and how it went. Typing `history`, `who changed what` or `security` finds it too.

On a server, **Your access** says what Lumovi's admins let you do, and why: see [Your access](/clusters/your-access). Typing `permissions` or `why can't I` finds it too. Admins also have **Access**, marked **Admin**, which opens the [Access pages](/server/access#the-access-pages).

<Tip>
  Some views answer to other words too: `prometheus` or `usage history` finds Metrics, `recommendations` or `over-provisioned` finds Right-sizing, `charts` finds Helm releases, and `crds` finds API resources.
</Tip>

The palette looks in the cluster you're in. In a [fleet](/clusters/fleet), to find a workload in any of its clusters, use **Find a workload** on the page of every cluster: it looks for Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets and CronJobs by name or namespace in all of them at once. There, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> goes to the search box that finds clusters.

## Go-to keys

Every main view has a two-key shortcut: press <kbd>G</kbd>, then a letter, within about a second. For example <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> for Pods, <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> for Deployments. The sidebar shows each view's keys when you hover it, and [Keyboard shortcuts](/reference/keyboard-shortcuts) lists them all.

## The sidebar

From top to bottom, the sidebar has:

- **The cluster switcher**: the cluster you're in, its Kubernetes version, and a lock when it's [read-only](/changes/read-only). Open it to switch clusters, in the desktop app or a [fleet](/clusters/fleet). See [Connect your clusters](/clusters/connect#switching-clusters).
- **Overview**, **Workloads**, **Pods**, **Metrics** (with its **Right-sizing** tab) and **Helm releases**. Workloads stays highlighted on the list of each workload kind.
- **Cluster**, **Network**, **Configuration**, **Storage** and **Access control**: Kubernetes' own kinds, like **Nodes**, **Services**, **Secrets**, **Volume Claims** and **Roles**. [Plugins](/custom-resources/plugins) that extend them sit with them when the cluster has them: **Cilium**, **Gateway API**, **Linkerd** and **Traefik** under Network, **Sealed Secrets** under Configuration, **Longhorn**, **Rook Ceph** and **Volume Snapshots** under Storage.
- **Pinned**: the kinds you pinned, in every cluster that serves them, then the [objects you pinned](#pin-an-object) in this cluster, each with its status. Pin a custom kind from the pin button above its list, or from **API resources**.
- **Plugins**: a page for each of the other tools the cluster runs, like **Argo CD**, **cert-manager** or **Karpenter**.
- **Custom resources**: the custom kinds you opened last in this cluster (up to five), then **API resources**, with how many custom kinds the cluster serves. Kinds you pinned, and kinds that belong to a plugin, aren't repeated here.

At the bottom are the theme, **AI assistants** (the sparkles, with a green dot while an [assistant](/assistants/overview) is connected; on a server, unless its administrator turned them off), **Audit log** (the scroll), **Admin: who may do what** (the shield, for Lumovi's [admins](/server/access)), your account when Lumovi runs [in your cluster](/server/overview), a link to Lumovi on GitHub, and the version you're running.

ReplicaSets and Autoscalers have lists of their own, but no place in the sidebar: find them with <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>.

## Narrowing a list

Lists of objects, the Workloads page and plugin pages all have the same three tools above them. **Helm releases** has the first two.

### Health

The chips count each [health level](/explore/health) in the list: **Failing**, **Warning**, **In progress**, **Healthy**, **Inactive** (some kinds use their own words, like **Running** for pods). Click one to show only those, and click more to add them. Click it again to clear it.

### Filter

Press <kbd>/</kbd>, or click the filter box, and type. It matches, without case:

- names and namespaces
- labels, written as `key=value`, like `app=checkout`
- for events, the reason, message and object's name
- for custom resources, what the list's columns show

On the Workloads page, the filter also matches each workload's kind and images. On a plugin's page, it matches each object's kind and what its status says, like the reason it failed.

<kbd>↓</kbd> moves from the filter into the list. <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves the filter.

### Label selector

The label selector is sent to the cluster, which does the filtering, so it's the way to narrow lists in big clusters. It takes Kubernetes' own syntax; press <kbd>↵</kbd> to apply it.

```text
app=web
app=web,tier!=cache
environment in (staging, production)
!canary
```

A list stops at 5,000 objects (or `LUMOVI_MAX_LIST_ITEMS`). When there are more, it says so, with **Filter by label**, because a selector lets the cluster send only the ones you want.

## Sorting and pages

- **Sort** by clicking a column's header, and again to reverse it. Not every column sorts: the name, status and age do, and so do numbers like restarts, CPU, memory, count and last seen, and the columns custom resources get from the cluster or a view.
- Lists start sorted by status, the most urgent first; kinds with no status by name, and events by when they were last seen. **Helm releases** starts sorted by name.
- With every column that sorts hidden, a list keeps its own order.
- **Pages** hold 100 rows, or 50, 250 or 500 (**Rows per page**). Use <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> for the previous and next page. **Helm releases** shows every release on one page.

## Columns

**Columns**, at the end of a list's toolbar, chooses what the list shows. So does a heading's right-click menu: **Hide this column**, or **Columns…** for the rest.

- **Show or hide** a column with its box, and **move** it by dragging, or with <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>↓</kbd>. A list's name column stays first.
- **Add a column from a label or an annotation** the listed objects carry: start typing its key, and the keys they carry are offered, the most common first. kubectl's own annotations aren't offered. An object without it shows a dash, and **Remove this column** takes it away again. Its values are the cluster's text: a character that can't be seen, or would turn the text round, shows as a mark, like `U+202E`.
- **Size a column** by dragging its heading's edge, or, with the heading focused, by 16 pixels with <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>→</kbd>. Double-click the edge, or press <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>0</kbd>, to give it its own width again. A column is never narrower than 64 pixels or its heading's first word, and the name never narrower than 160 pixels.
- **A list never scrolls sideways**, at any width, beside the panel or not. Where there's no room, the least important columns give way first; then the status narrows to its icon, in its colour, with its word in a tooltip; then the other columns give way, from the right; and last the name narrows, down to 140 pixels, cut in its middle so similar names stay told apart. In **Events**, the message stays, the type narrows to its icon, and the reason gives way. Where you chose or moved columns, they give way in your order instead, the last first, the status narrowing at its turn. **Columns** says how many aren't drawn, and the menu marks each "doesn't fit".
- **Headings stay** at the top as the list scrolls, and the rows don't show through them.

On Windows and Linux, <kbd>⌥</kbd> is <kbd>Alt</kbd>. What you choose is kept for that kind in that cluster: in the desktop app, and on a server in the browser you chose it in. **Reset** brings back Lumovi's own columns.

## Copy and download

- **Copy**, the first button in the bar of picked rows, or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>C</kbd> in the list, copies the picked rows as the list shows them: a heading row, then a row each, with a tab between columns. Each value is whole, with its line breaks and tabs as spaces.
- **Download CSV**, in the **Columns** menu, saves every row the filters let through, not only the page you're on, with the columns you show. Values are whole, times are in UTC (`2026-10-13T09:12:03Z`), and numbers have no units: CPU in cores and memory in bytes, as the headings say. The file is named for the kind, the namespace (or the cluster, with several picked) and the day, like `pods-shop-2026-10-13.csv`. It's UTF-8 with lines that end in CRLF, as spreadsheets read it.
- In both, a value that a spreadsheet would read as a formula (one starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return) starts with `'`, so it's shown as text and never run.

In the desktop app you choose where the file goes, and then **Show in Finder** (**Show in Explorer** on Windows, **Show in folder** on Linux) opens its folder. On a server, the browser downloads it.

## Pin an object

To keep an object at hand, pin it: **Pin** on its page, or <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>P</kbd>; the pin at the end of its row in a list; or **Pin** in the command palette while it's open. Up to 20 in a cluster. An Event can't be pinned: it's gone within the hour.

Pinned objects stand in the sidebar under **Pinned**, after the kinds you pinned, each with its name and its status, in the order you pinned them. <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>9</kbd> open the first nine. (On Windows and Linux, <kbd>⌥</kbd> is <kbd>Alt</kbd>. A browser on Linux keeps <kbd>Alt</kbd> and a number for its own tabs: open them from the sidebar or the palette there.)

- **Move one** by dragging it in the sidebar, or with <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>↓</kbd> in the palette's **Pinned** group.
- **Unpin one** with **Pinned** on its page, the pin in its row, or <kbd>⌫</kbd> in the palette's **Pinned** group. **Undo** puts it back where it was.
- **One that's gone** says "Deployment cart in shop isn’t in the cluster any more.", and **one you can't read** any more says so. Either opens its kind's list, filtered to its name, and has its own unpin button in the sidebar.

On a phone, the menu's drawer holds the pins, and a long press opens a pin's sheet, to open or unpin it.

## Saved views

A list's filter, health, labels, sort, columns and namespaces can be saved as a view, with a name, to come back to it. **Views**, in the list's toolbar, saves what the list shows now (**Save view…**), and lists the kind's saved views to open. The palette has them too: the views of the list you're on, then the others.

A view opened and then changed says so on the button, like **Ready · changed**, and **Save changes to “Ready”** keeps the change. Rename, move and delete views in the **Views** menu; a delete has **Undo**. Names are a kind's own: two views of Deployments can't have the same name. Up to 20 views of one kind, and 50 in a cluster. A view of a kind the cluster doesn't serve any more says so.

## Where pins and views are kept

- **In the desktop app**, on your computer, for each cluster.
- **On a server**, with who's signed in, wherever Lumovi keeps its state: the chart's Secret (`auth.keepSessions`), `LUMOVI_DATA_DIR`, or memory, where a restart loses them. So they're the same in any browser you sign in from. Everyone who signs in with the same token shares one set.

A server keeps up to 32 kB of a person's in one cluster, 96 kB of theirs in all, and 400 kB for everyone. A person's are let go after 180 days without use. When everyone's would pass 400 kB, those of clusters the server doesn't show now go first, the longest unread first. Past a limit, a pin or a view isn't saved, and Lumovi says which, like "This server keeps no more of your pins and views in this cluster. Unpin or delete some to save this."

## Moving through a list

| Keys | Does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd>, or <kbd>J</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> | Move through the rows |
| <kbd>Home</kbd> <kbd>End</kbd> | First and last row |
| <kbd>PgUp</kbd> <kbd>PgDn</kbd> | Ten rows at a time |
| <kbd>↵</kbd> | Open the row |
| <kbd>X</kbd>, <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>X</kbd> | Select the row, or a range |
| <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> | Select every row on the page |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Clear the selection, then close the panel |

## Back and Forward

The filter, health chips, label selector, sort, page and the open object are all part of the page's address. **Back** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>←</kbd>) and **Forward** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>→</kbd>) bring each of them back, like in a browser. On **Helm releases**, only the open release is part of the address.

<Note>
  **In your cluster:** these addresses are real links. Copy one from the address bar to share a filtered list, or an open pod, with a teammate.
</Note>
