Two habits cover most of it: press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> to go anywhere, and press <kbd>/</kbd> to filter the list you're on. On Windows and Linux, use <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> instead of <kbd>⌘</kbd>.

## The command palette

Press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> (or click **Search…** in the header) and type. Case doesn't matter. Views and commands match when every word you type appears in them, in any order, so `vol cl` finds Volume Claims. Objects match when their name contains what you typed.

Results come in groups:

| Group | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Actions** | When an object is open: everything you can do to it. Actions you can't take are shown, disabled. |
| **Objects** | Once you've typed two characters: objects whose names match, among those Lumovi has already loaded in this cluster (up to 20). |
| **Go to** | Every view: the overview, Workloads, Metrics and [Right-sizing](/metrics/right-sizing), Helm releases, API resources, the page of each [add-on](/custom-resources/add-ons) the cluster has (marked **Add-on**), and every kind the cluster serves, found by name, kind, short name or API group. Custom resources included. |
| **Namespace** | **All namespaces**, and every namespace. |
| **Clusters** | Every cluster in your kubeconfig, and **All clusters**. When Lumovi runs in your cluster, only in a [fleet](/clusters/fleet): every cluster you may see, and **All clusters**, the fleet's page of clusters. |
| **Help & appearance** | **Create from YAML**, **New terminal** (in the desktop app), **Metrics source…**, **AI assistants…**, **Audit log**, **Access** (for Lumovi's admins, on a server), **Your access** (on a server), **Keyboard shortcuts**, **Use system theme**, **Use light theme**, **Use dark theme**, and **Make** _cluster_ **read-only** or **Allow changes to** _cluster_. The last one isn't there when `LUMOVI_READ_ONLY` makes every cluster read-only. |

Move with <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd>, press <kbd>↵</kbd> to pick, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close.

**New terminal** opens a [terminal](/debug/terminal) in the dock at the bottom of the page: your own shell, with kubectl pointed at the cluster you're in and its namespace. The palette shows its key, <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>&#96;</kbd>, which works anywhere in a cluster. Typing `kubectl` or `shell` finds it too.

**AI assistants…** opens the **AI assistants** page: connect an [assistant](/assistants/overview) (turning them on, in the desktop app), see the ones you allowed on a [server](/assistants/server), say [what they may do](/assistants/permissions), and see what they did. Typing `mcp`, `claude` or `cursor` finds it too.

**Audit log** opens the [audit log](/audit/overview): what was done through Lumovi, by whom, and how it went. Typing `history`, `who changed what` or `security` finds it too.

On a server, **Your access** says what Lumovi's admins let you do, and why: see [Your access](/clusters/your-access). Typing `permissions` or `why can't I` finds it too. Admins also have **Access**, marked **Admin**, which opens the [Access pages](/server/access#the-access-pages).

<Tip>
  Some views answer to other words too: `prometheus` or `usage history` finds Metrics, `recommendations` or `over-provisioned` finds Right-sizing, `charts` finds Helm releases, and `crds` finds API resources.
</Tip>

The palette looks in the cluster you're in. In a [fleet](/clusters/fleet), to find a workload in any of its clusters, use **Find a workload** on the page of every cluster: it looks for Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets and CronJobs by name or namespace in all of them at once. There, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> goes to the search box that finds clusters.

## Go-to keys

Every main view has a two-key shortcut: press <kbd>G</kbd>, then a letter, within about a second. For example <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> for Pods, <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> for Deployments. The sidebar shows each view's keys when you hover it, and [Keyboard shortcuts](/reference/keyboard-shortcuts) lists them all.

## The sidebar

From top to bottom, the sidebar has:

- **The cluster switcher**: the cluster you're in, its Kubernetes version, and a lock when it's [read-only](/changes/read-only). Open it to switch clusters, in the desktop app or a [fleet](/clusters/fleet). See [Connect your clusters](/clusters/connect#switching-clusters).
- **Overview**, **Workloads**, **Pods**, **Metrics** (with its **Right-sizing** tab) and **Helm releases**. Workloads stays highlighted on the list of each workload kind.
- **Cluster**, **Network**, **Configuration** and **Storage**: Kubernetes' own kinds, like **Nodes**, **Services**, **Secrets** and **Volume Claims**. [Add-ons](/custom-resources/add-ons) that extend them sit with them when the cluster has them: **Cilium**, **Gateway API**, **Linkerd** and **Traefik** under Network, **Sealed Secrets** under Configuration, **Longhorn**, **Rook Ceph** and **Volume Snapshots** under Storage.
- **Pinned**: the kinds you pinned, in every cluster that serves them. Pin a custom kind from the pin button above its list, or from **API resources**.
- **Add-ons**: a page for each of the other tools the cluster runs, like **Argo CD**, **cert-manager** or **Karpenter**.
- **Custom resources**: the custom kinds you opened last in this cluster (up to five), then **API resources**, with how many custom kinds the cluster serves. Kinds you pinned, and kinds that belong to an add-on, aren't repeated here.

At the bottom are the theme, **AI assistants** (the sparkles, with a green dot while an [assistant](/assistants/overview) is connected; on a server, unless its administrator turned them off), **Audit log** (the scroll), **Admin: who may do what** (the shield, for Lumovi's [admins](/server/access)), your account when Lumovi runs [in your cluster](/server/overview), a link to Lumovi on GitHub, and the version you're running.

ReplicaSets and Autoscalers have lists of their own, but no place in the sidebar: find them with <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>.

## Narrowing a list

Lists of objects, the Workloads page and add-on pages all have the same three tools above them. **Helm releases** has the first two.

### Health

The chips count each [health level](/explore/health) in the list: **Failing**, **Warning**, **In progress**, **Healthy**, **Inactive** (some kinds use their own words, like **Running** for pods). Click one to show only those, and click more to add them. Click it again to clear it.

### Filter

Press <kbd>/</kbd>, or click the filter box, and type. It matches, without case:

- names and namespaces
- labels, written as `key=value`, like `app=checkout`
- for events, the reason, message and object's name
- for custom resources, what the list's columns show

On the Workloads page, the filter also matches each workload's kind and images. On an add-on's page, it matches each object's kind and what its status says, like the reason it failed.

<kbd>↓</kbd> moves from the filter into the list. <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves the filter.

### Label selector

The label selector is sent to the cluster, which does the filtering, so it's the way to narrow lists in big clusters. It takes Kubernetes' own syntax; press <kbd>↵</kbd> to apply it.

```text
app=web
app=web,tier!=cache
environment in (staging, production)
!canary
```

A list stops at 5,000 objects (or `LUMOVI_MAX_LIST_ITEMS`). When there are more, it says so, with **Filter by label**, because a selector lets the cluster send only the ones you want.

## Sorting and pages

- **Sort** by clicking a column's header, and again to reverse it. Not every column sorts: the name, status and age do, and so do numbers like restarts, CPU, memory, count and last seen, and the columns custom resources get from the cluster or a view.
- Lists start sorted by status, the most urgent first; kinds with no status by name, and events by when they were last seen. **Helm releases** starts sorted by name.
- **Pages** hold 100 rows, or 50, 250 or 500 (**Rows per page**). Use <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> for the previous and next page. **Helm releases** shows every release on one page.

## Moving through a list

| Keys | Does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd>, or <kbd>J</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> | Move through the rows |
| <kbd>Home</kbd> <kbd>End</kbd> | First and last row |
| <kbd>PgUp</kbd> <kbd>PgDn</kbd> | Ten rows at a time |
| <kbd>↵</kbd> | Open the row |
| <kbd>X</kbd>, <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>X</kbd> | Select the row, or a range |
| <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> | Select every row on the page |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Clear the selection, then close the panel |

## Back and Forward

The filter, health chips, label selector, sort, page and the open object are all part of the page's address. **Back** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>←</kbd>) and **Forward** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>→</kbd>) bring each of them back, like in a browser. On **Helm releases**, only the open release is part of the address.

<Note>
  **In your cluster:** these addresses are real links. Copy one from the address bar to share a filtered list, or an open pod, with a teammate.
</Note>
