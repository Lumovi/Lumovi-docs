Most Kubernetes tools are either a terminal or a wall of tables. Lumovi surfaces problems first, shows live usage next to what's allocatable, and gets out of your way. When something needs a hand (a scale, a restart, a rollback, a drain) it's a click or a keystroke away, with guard rails.

## Two ways to run it

It's the same app either way. Pick the one that fits, or use both.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="On your desktop" icon="monitor" href="/get-started/desktop" cta="Install the app">
    A native app for macOS, Windows and Linux. It reads your kubeconfig, like `kubectl`, and opens any cluster in it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="In your cluster" icon="server" href="/get-started/in-your-cluster" cta="Install with Helm">
    One Helm chart serves Lumovi as a web dashboard. Your team opens a link, signs in, and sees what their own RBAC allows.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Tip>
  Joining a team that already runs Lumovi? You don't need to install anything. See [Signing in](/get-started/sign-in).
</Tip>

## Made for the day something breaks

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Problems come first" icon="arrow-up-narrow-wide">
    Lists sort by health, so you won't scroll past 400 healthy pods to find the one that isn't. The overview lists what needs attention and the latest warnings. Statuses always come with an icon and a label, never color alone. [How health works](/explore/health).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Nothing surprising" icon="eye">
    Every change names the cluster and shows the equivalent `kubectl` command. YAML edits are checked by the cluster with a dry run and shown as a diff before they're saved. [Changing things safely](/changes/safely).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Hard to do by accident" icon="lock">
    Lumovi asks the cluster what you're allowed to do before offering it. Risky deletes, and deletes in a cluster named like production, ask you to type the name first. [Read-only mode](/changes/read-only) turns changes off entirely.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Calm when things go wrong" icon="cloud-off">
    A lost connection shows a banner and keeps the last data on screen, instead of a blank window. Every error says what happened and what to try. [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Built for big clusters" icon="layers">
    Lists load in chunks of 500 and are paginated and virtualized, so thousands of pods stay smooth. A label selector narrows a list on the server.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Requirements

Live usage needs [metrics-server](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/metrics-server), and usage history needs a Prometheus or VictoriaMetrics in the cluster. Everything else works without them, and without metrics-server, Lumovi shows requests and limits against capacity instead.

## Free and open source

Lumovi is free under the Apache 2.0 license, with no telemetry and no accounts. It talks to your clusters, and otherwise only to GitHub to look for new versions (which you can turn off) and to Artifact Hub when you search it for charts. See [Privacy and security](/reference/privacy-and-security).
