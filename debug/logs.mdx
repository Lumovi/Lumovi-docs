Logs stream as they're written, like `kubectl logs -f`. For a workload, you get every pod's logs in one list, in the order the lines were written, each marked with its pod.

## Where logs are

Open an object, then its **Logs** tab:

- **A pod** shows its own containers' logs.
- **A Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, ReplicaSet, Job or Service** shows the logs of all its pods, merged. So do custom workloads that scale pods, like Argo Rollouts.

Pods that start later join in, and a pod that goes away keeps the lines it wrote. Nodes don't have a Logs tab: that would be everything on the node.

The [audit log](/audit/overview) records whose logs you read, pod by pod and container by container, once every ten minutes at most.

<Note>
  **In your cluster:** where your [access](/clusters/your-access) doesn't let you read logs, the **Logs** tab says **You can't read logs here**, and why.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Need the command instead? Right-click a pod in any list and choose **Copy kubectl logs**.
</Tip>

## Pick what to show

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Container** | Which container's logs to show. Init containers come first. With more than one container, **All containers** streams them all. It starts on the first container of the first pod. |
| **Show** | How far back to start: **Last 100 lines**, **Last 500 lines** (the default), **Last 2,000 lines**, **Last 5 minutes**, **Last hour**, **Last day**, or **Since…** a time you type: see [From any time](#from-any-time). |
| **Previous container** | The logs of the container's previous run, like `kubectl logs --previous`. Use it for a container that crashed and restarted: its last words are usually in the run before. These don't follow, since that run has ended. |

## Read them

| Toggle | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Follow** | Keeps streaming new lines as they're written. On by default. |
| **Timestamps** | Shows when each line was written, in your local time. On by default; on a phone it's off, under **More**. |
| **Wrap lines** | Wraps long lines instead of scrolling sideways. |

When you scroll up to read, the view stops jumping to new lines, and **Jump to latest** takes you back down.

- **Colors.** The colors and styles apps print with ANSI escape codes show as they would in a terminal: 16, 256 and true colors, bold, dim, italic and underline. Other escape codes are left out.
- **JSON lines.** Structured lines show their level as a badge, then the message, then the other fields, dimmed, as `key=value`.

## From any time

**Show → Since…** takes a time ago, like `90m`, `2h` or `3d`, up to 30 days, or a time of day or a date and time, like `14:30` or `2026-10-11 09:00`, in your own time. Before you choose **Show**, it reads it back to you: "From 12:58 today, 90 minutes ago, your time (UTC+2)." A time still to come, or one it can't read, says so. The picker then names it, like **Last 90 minutes** or **Since 09:00 yesterday**. On a phone, a tap fills in `15m`, `2h`, `6h` or today's `00:00`.

A span keeps at most 20,000 lines. With more, it shows the last 20,000 and says so: "Showing the last 20,000 lines since 09:00 yesterday. Pick a later time to see every line from it." With several containers, each keeps its own share of them, at least 2,000, so a busy one doesn't crowd out a quiet one; a note says which were cut.

## Search and filter

- **Search** finds lines that contain what you type, ignoring case. Matches are highlighted, and the field counts them: **12 matches**, or **3 of 12** once you step to one with <kbd>↵</kbd> (<kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>↵</kbd> goes back), or the arrows beside it. <kbd>Esc</kbd> clears it.
- **Three toggles in the field** change how it searches, each with a key that works while you're typing:

  | Toggle | Key (Mac) | Key (Windows, Linux) | Does |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | **Regular expression** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>R</kbd> | <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>R</kbd> | Reads what you type as a JavaScript regular expression. Spaces at its ends count. |
  | **Match case** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>C</kbd> | <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>C</kbd> | Tells capitals from small letters. |
  | **Show lines without a match** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>X</kbd> | <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>X</kbd> | Shows the lines that don't match instead, and counts them. |

  Where your keyboard types a letter with that key, like `ć` with <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>C</kbd> on a Polish Mac, the key types it, and the toggle's tooltip leaves the key out. Lumovi remembers the toggles for every Logs view; what you typed stays with the view while the page is open, never shared with another view.
- **An expression that isn't one** says why, like "This isn't a regular expression: a ( isn't closed.", and the lines stay as the last search left them, dimmed. So does **a search that takes too long**: one that takes more than a second over the lines is stopped, with "This expression took too long on 20,000 lines, so Lumovi stopped it. Try a simpler one." It runs apart from the page, so the page never stops answering while it does.
- **Errors** and **Warnings** appear when there are such lines, with a count. Press one to show only those lines; with neither pressed, every line shows.
- **A chip per pod** appears when there are several pods, with the part of the names they share left out. Press one to leave that pod's lines out; it shows crossed out until you press it again.

On a phone, the toggles and the arrows get a row of their own under the field while you search.

### Lines over time

Wider than a phone, a strip under the search shows how many lines were written over time, errors and warnings marked in each bar; hover a bar for its time and counts. Drag across it to show only the lines of that stretch: a chip says which, like **14:02–14:09**, and its **×** shows all times again. Picking a stretch stops **Follow**, so new lines don't pass it by. With the keyboard, move along the bars with the arrow keys, hold <kbd>⇧</kbd> to make a stretch, and press <kbd>↵</kbd>; <kbd>Esc</kbd> lets it go. It's drawn once the lines span at least two minutes.

Lumovi reads a line's level from its `level` field when it has one (`"level":"warn"` in JSON, or `level=WARN`). Otherwise it looks for words in the line:

| Level | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Error | error, fatal, panic, crit, critical, exception |
| Warning | warn, warning |

## Copy and download

**Copy logs** and **Download** take the lines you're looking at, filters applied, one per line, each starting with the time it was written. When the lines come from several pods or containers, each also names its pod and container:

```text
2026-10-02T09:14:03.512345678Z checkout-7d9f8-hqnnn/app payment provider timed out
```

Downloads are named after the object, like `checkout.log`, and end in `-filtered`, like `checkout-filtered.log`, when they're only part of the log: a search, a level, a pod left out, or a stretch of time. The desktop app asks where to save them, starting in your Downloads folder; in a browser, they download like any other file.

## Limits

- **20,000 lines.** The view keeps the latest 20,000 lines, and drops the oldest. It says so when it does: "Showing the latest 20,000 lines." A span from **Show** is cut the same way: see [From any time](#from-any-time).
- **30 streams.** A workload's logs stream from at most 30 containers at once. With more, a note says so: pick a container, or open a single pod, to see the rest.

## When the stream drops

While following, if a container restarts or the connection drops, Lumovi tries again after a few seconds, picking up from the last line it has, without repeating lines. The view says **Trying again…** meanwhile. Problems that trying again won't fix, like expired credentials or missing permissions, are shown instead.

<Note>
  Reading logs needs `get` on `pods/log` in the pod's namespace. Logs work in [read-only](/changes/read-only) clusters.
</Note>
