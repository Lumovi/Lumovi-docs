Restarting ten pods, cordoning three nodes or cleaning up a batch of finished jobs takes one action, not ten. Pick the rows, choose what to do, and watch each object's progress.

## Pick rows

Selecting works in a kind's list, and across kinds on the **Workloads** page and an [add-on's](/custom-resources/add-ons) page. Events can't be selected, since nothing is done to them by hand, and neither can Helm releases or the lists inside the detail panel.

| To | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Pick a row | Tick its checkbox, or press <kbd>X</kbd> on the focused row |
| Pick a range | <kbd>Shift</kbd>-click a checkbox, or press <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>X</kbd> |
| Pick every row on the page | Tick the checkbox in the header, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> on Windows and Linux) |
| Clear the selection | <kbd>Esc</kbd>, or the **×** in the selection bar |

A bar at the bottom of the list says how many rows you picked, and offers the actions that apply to at least one of them.

<Tip>
  Narrow the list first, with the filter (<kbd>/</kbd>), a health chip or a label selector, then press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> to pick everything that's left. See [Finding things](/explore/finding-things).
</Tip>

## What you can do

| Action | For | Like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Restart** | Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, and pods a controller manages | `kubectl rollout restart` for workloads; `kubectl delete` for pods, which their controllers replace |
| **Cordon**, **Uncordon** | Nodes | `kubectl cordon`, `kubectl uncordon` |
| **Suspend**, **Resume** | CronJobs and Jobs | `kubectl patch … -p '{"spec":{"suspend":true}}'` |
| **Delete** | Any kind | `kubectl delete` |

If an action applies to only some of the rows you picked, the dialog says how many it leaves out, like "Left out: 2 of the selected, which restart doesn't apply to." Those rows are left alone.

## Run it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the action">
    Choose it in the selection bar. The dialog lists every object it will change, and the equivalent `kubectl` commands: one line for each namespace and kind.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Confirm, and the changes run, four at a time. Each object shows its own progress, and the reason if it fails.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Retry what failed">
    If some couldn't be changed, the dialog says how many ("2 of 12 couldn't be changed; see why below.") and the button becomes **Retry failed**. It runs the action again, only for those.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When everything succeeds, a notification confirms it, like "Restarted 12 pods".

## Deleting many at once

Bulk delete removes each object the way a single delete does by default: anything an object owns is deleted after it. There's no force option, and no choice of what happens to owned objects. For those, delete one at a time. See [Actions by kind](/changes/actions).

Some deletes ask you to type what's being done before they run:

- Deleting Namespaces, Nodes, PersistentVolumes, PersistentVolumeClaims, StorageClasses or CustomResourceDefinitions.
- Deleting anything in a cluster whose name looks like production. See [Changing things safely](/changes/safely#hard-to-do-by-accident).

You type the action and what it's for, like `delete namespaces`, `delete volume claims` or, on the Workloads page, `delete workloads`.

## Good to know

- **No undo.** Changes made together can't be undone from their notification. Each one is in the **Activity** log, with its command.
- **Permissions are checked by the cluster.** Bulk actions don't check your permissions before they start. Objects you can't change fail with the cluster's reason, and the rest go ahead.
- **Read-only clusters.** In a [read-only](/changes/read-only) cluster, the actions in the selection bar are disabled. On a server, so are they when your [access](/clusters/your-access) doesn't let you change one of the objects chosen, saying why.
